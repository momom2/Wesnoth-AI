# The imitation corpus, version 2 (2026-09-26)

Every reference checkpoint learned by imitation of `replays_dataset_imitation`
(17,019 games). A crawl of its labels on 2026-09-26 (BACKLOG.md "The imitation
corpus's labels") found errors that only a re-extraction fixes. Branch
`feature/corpus-v2` builds the corpus again under corrected rules; it does not
rebuild it. This note says what changes, how much, and what a retrain on the
new corpus would test.

## Quickstart: the rebuild

From the laptop's repository root (the raw replays live only there):

    python tools/stage_raw_corpus.py --out /tmp/raw_corpus.tar \
        --upload tier-b/corpus_v2/raw_corpus_20260926.tar
    python tools/stage_code.py --out /tmp/stage.tar.gz \
        --script scripts/corpus_v2_rebuild_box.sh --upload tier-b/staging/stage_DATE.tar.gz
    python scripts/rent_box.py create OFFER_ID --onstart corpus_v2_rebuild_box.sh \
        --stage tier-b/staging/stage_DATE.tar.gz --hours 1

The box runs the corpus tests, unpacks the raw replays, runs
`tools/build_imitation_dataset.py` and uploads the corpus to
`tier-b/corpus_v2_20260926/replays_dataset_imitation_v2.tar.gz` with its
manifest, outcomes and build log. Any machine holding the raw replays can run
the builder itself: `python tools/build_imitation_dataset.py --raw-root .
--out DIR --workers N`.

Cost: 0.23-0.34 s per candidate on one laptop core (runs of 150 and 250 games),
so the 19,367 candidates of the dispositions ledger take 1.2-1.8 core-hours: 5
to 7 minutes on 16 cores. The inputs are 0.23 GiB, the corpus about 60 MiB.
The box needs no GPU.

## What changes

Numbers are from random samples of today's manifest unless said otherwise.

| Rule | Code | Reach |
|---|---|---|
| A move the engine stopped short is labelled with the hex its player clicked | `replay_extract` keeps the clicked hex as the move's order; `replay_dataset.move_label_hex` | 1,437 of 25,034 player moves (150 games) |
| A game ends at the first action after a surrender, or at the first action of a side's turn its opponent took | `tools/replay_control.py` | 61 of 300 games cut; 3,015 of their 47,199 winner actions |
| The outcome: leader death, else the surrendering side loses, unless it was ahead on material | `tools/replay_outcome.py` | 6 of 550 winners flip, 10 games lose their label |
| Games in which the AI played side 1 or 2 leave the corpus | `build_imitation_dataset.quarantine_of`, config `quarantine_ai_player_sides` | 53 of 600 games |
| Reloaded games deduplicate | `replay_dataset.match_key` reads 30 commands | 9 clusters, 19 games, 10 dropped (whole corpus) |
| WL_Troll_Toll finds its scenario | `scenario_events.scenario_id_of_cfg` | 5 games |
| Each label's slots are checked against its command | `encode_worker.label_slot_mismatch` | 0 of 22,318 pairs (84 games, both bases) |
| Pre-encoded records carry the corpus version | `replay_dataset.corpus_version_of`, `preencode_corpus.vocab_fingerprint` | records of version 1 refuse a version 2 corpus |

### Stopped moves

A replay's [move] carries the player's order, the path to the hex clicked, and
its checkup the hex where the unit stopped (docs/wesnoth_rules.md "Replay [move]
checkup: final_hex and stopped_early"). The extractor cut the path there, and
the label took the path's end. The path still ends at the stop, so the state
is rebuilt as before; the clicked hex travels with the move as
`{"clicked": [x, y], "stopped_early": bool}`, and the label names it when the
engine says the unit stopped early and the clicked hex is one the unit can end
a move on in the pre-move observable state (the legality mask's move targets).
The simulator does not stop a move on sighting an enemy, so that hex is where
the unit goes in our games.

On 150 games: 1,753 of 25,034 player moves stopped short of the clicked hex.

- 1,437 stopped early with the clicked hex in reach: labelled with it. In 967
  of them the player's next command moves the same unit on to that hex.
- 305 had `stopped_early=no`: the order was longer than a turn, and the unit
  went as far as the order planned for this turn (in 90 games, none of 119
  such clicked hexes was in the unit's reach). The stop stays the label;
  these were right before. The crawl's 192,575 counted them too, so about 83%
  of those change.
- 11 stopped early with the clicked hex out of reach (an order longer than a
  turn, cut short as well): the stop stays the label.

The clicked hex is in the relevant-set basis whenever it is in reach (1,437 of
1,437). Records of version 1 carry no order and keep their labels.

### Where a game ends

After a surrender the server hands the side to the other player, and a
player who leaves or disconnects hands it to the host; the replay keeps
recording, and one person can then play both sides
(docs/wesnoth_rules.md "Surrender and control changes in a server replay").
The corpus game now ends at the first action after the first surrender, and at
the first action of a player side's turn while that side is held by the other
side's player. A player who comes back before their turn is not cut, nor is a
side a third person takes over and plays on. A game in which one name holds
both sides from the start (a player alone on both) has no play and is
quarantined.

On 300 games: 49 cut at a surrender (1,910 winner-side actions, 819
loser-side), 9 at a side played by its opponent (771 and 446), 3 with one name
on both sides (334 and 226). The rule follows who holds each side, so a
disconnect counts like a leave: in Sablestone Delta 867 the host played both
sides for three turns after the other player disconnected. And it keeps what the
players played on: in Caves of the Basilisk 104459 the player who
disconnected took their side back and the game went on for 22 turns.

### The outcome labeller

`tools/replay_outcome.py` replaces the script behind
`training/logs/replay_outcomes.jsonl.gz`, which is in no commit. Over the
record as cut:

1. leader_death: a side that started with a leader has none; it lost.
2. surrender: the side the server names as surrendering lost, unless its
   material (non-leader units' cost times HP fraction, plus gold) was more than
   1.05 times the other side's: then abandoned, with no winner. The side comes
   from the [surrender] command (0-based) and from the surrendering player's
   side; they agreed in 108 of 108 surrender games.
3. left: the record was cut where a side's turn was taken by its opponent;
   that side lost.
4. inconclusive otherwise.

`configs/imitation.json` keeps leader_death and surrender. Against today's
labels, on 550 games: leader_death 351, all agreeing; surrender 164, 158
agreeing and 6 flipping; abandoned 10; left 22, all agreeing (and left out);
3 with one name on both sides. On 150 of the crawl's 433 games whose label
names the surrendering side: 108 become abandoned (the surrendering side was
ahead on material by more than 5%) and 42 flip.

### Games against the AI

53 of 600 sampled games had the AI on side 1 or 2 from the start (25 of them on
Micro Isar); the AI is the labelled winner in 12, so the policy loss imitated
the default RCA AI there. They held 7.9% of the sample's winner actions.
`quarantine_ai_player_sides` (on) leaves them out.

### Duplicates

The match key reads the first 30 commands (a game reloaded from a save shares
its history up to the reload), and on to the first command carrying an engine
seed when those 30 have none (5 of 2,000 games), up to the former 200. Over
the whole existing corpus it finds the crawl's 9 clusters, 19 games, of the
same two players each; 10 leave, none across the holdout split.

### The value corpus's surrender labels

`tools/build_value_corpus.py` read the [surrender] command's `side_number` as a
side number. It is the surrendering client's 0-based team: all 1,334 surrender
games of `replays_dataset/value_corpus_index.jsonl` (6,621 games) name the
surrendering side as the winner (300 checked against their replays), and a
surrender by side 1 was never read. The builder is fixed; that corpus is not
rebuilt here.

## What a retrain on it would test

The reference recipe (obs8's: relevant set, terrain multi-hot, fog gate, one
pass, its seed) on version 2 against `obs8`, 800 decisive games at
`raw:t0+eo-1.5`. It tests the corpus as one factor: about 6% of move labels
change, about 6% of winner actions leave with the cuts and 8% with the AI
games, and about 3% of games lose or change their winner (16 of 550).
Splitting it (labels alone, cuts alone) would take two more retrains.

- The holdout split keeps its rule (sha1 of the ledger path), but games leave
  and join it, so holdout numbers do not compare across the two versions.
- Pre-encoded records must be made again (`preencode_corpus` refuses to mix
  versions).
- Expected size: about 85% of today's games stay (the AI games, the left and
  the abandoned ones leave). Of the 2,348 candidates today's ledger left out,
  the new labeller keeps 4 of 150 sampled (91 inconclusive, 24 left, 25 AI
  games, 4 one-name, 1 abandoned, 1 fog-off shroud-on).
- The observation retrain of 2026-09-24 cost about 6.4 box-hours and $4.3.

Rejected: cutting at "has left the game." whether or not the other player
takes the side, because a returning player's game is cut with it and a
disconnect is missed (the two examples above).
