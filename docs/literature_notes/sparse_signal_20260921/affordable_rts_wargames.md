# RL on affordable RTS and turn-based wargame platforms: what they did about sparse rewards on small budgets

Scope: Gym-microRTS and later microRTS competition entries; the NPS Atlatl hex wargame and other military-simulation wargames; published AI work on The Battle for Wesnoth; CivRealm/Freeciv, Lux AI, Polytopia (Tribes), Fire Emblem-like tactics; Advance Wars. Publication years are given with each source. Numbers labelled "approximate reading of Figure N" came from a summarizer reading the paper's HTML, not from a table, and should be re-checked against the PDF before being quoted as constraints.

Target application reminder for the report writer: two-player turn-based hex wargame (Wesnoth), bit-exact simulator, strong imitation seed from ~17,000 human games, sparse win/loss/capped outcome, tens of dollars of single-GPU rental per experiment. Transfers to that setting are marked as inferences.

## Key question 1: Gym-microRTS (Huang et al. 2021) and later microRTS entries: rewards, action space, masking, opponents, ablations

### Takeaway
Gym-microRTS reached state-of-the-art microRTS play in ~60 GPU-hours by (a) a hand-weighted shaped reward, (b) invalid-action masking with a composed (multi-head) action space, and (c) training against a fixed pool of scripted bots rather than self-play (their self-play variants underperformed). The competition-winning successor (RAISocketAI, 2023) kept the shaped reward but annealed it to pure win/loss across a schedule, bootstrapped by behavior cloning from scripted bots and used 50% past-self opponents; its ablations put most of the gain on per-map fine-tuning, and show BC+PPO fine-tuning (88%) far above BC alone (71%).

### Cited Findings

**Gym-microRTS (Huang, Ontañón, Bamford, Grela; IEEE CoG 2021)**
- Best bot "can defeat every µRTS bot we tested from the past µRTS competitions when working in a single-map setting" with "about 60 hours of training using a single machine (one GPU, three vCPU, 16GB RAM)" — [arXiv 2105.13807 abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.13807)
- Shaped reward, paid at the frame the event initiates: win +10, draw 0, loss −10, harvest resource +1, produce worker +1, construct building +0.2, valid attack action +1, produce combat unit +4. Sparse reward: win +1, draw 0, loss −1 — [ar5iv HTML of 2105.13807](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2105.13807)
- Action space: UAS (unit action simulation: pick a unit via an h×w source mask, then its action via a 78-scalar unit-action mask, iterating with simulated intermediate states until every unit has an action) versus Gridnet (one forward pass emits an (h, w, 79) tensor, every cell's action issued simultaneously, invalid ones ignored) — [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2105.13807)
- Invalid-action masking replaces invalid logits with M = −1×10^8; "full" masking (every head masked) versus "partial" masking — [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2105.13807)
- Training: PPO (OpenAI Baselines implementation), 24 parallel environments, 256 steps/env (UAS) or 512 (Gridnet), 300M total steps, one g4dn.xlarge GPU, 37–117 hours per run — [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2105.13807)
- Opponent schedule: baseline is a single scripted opponent, CoacAI (2020 competition champion); "diverse opponents" allocates the 24 envs as 18 vs CoacAI, 2 vs RandomBiasedAI, 2 vs WorkerRush, 2 vs LightRush — [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2105.13807)
- Self-play: two variants tested in Appendix C "underperformed" and were left out of the main results — [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2105.13807)
- Ablation (approximate reading of Figure 4): PPO baseline ~30% win rate; + full invalid-action masking ~75–80%; partial masking ~35–40%; + diverse opponents ~85% (Gridnet) / ~80% (UAS); + IMPALA-CNN ~88% (UAS); + encoder-decoder ~87% (Gridnet). Authors: action composition plus invalid-action masking "allowed us to bootstrap an initial agent that could compete on the 16×16 map" — [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2105.13807)
- Shaped vs sparse (Figure 7): agents optimize the shaped return with low variance while the sparse win/loss return shows high variance; the authors flag that shaped rewards can incentivize behaviors that raise shaped return without winning — [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2105.13807)
- Gridnet reaches higher shaped returns (more harvesting, more units) than UAS with similar sparse returns; Gridnet agents learned 3-worker harvesting not seen in the scripted bots — [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2105.13807)
- Final evaluation: 11 competition bots + 2 baselines, 100 games each, 4000 ticks; best config "ppo + coacai + invalid action masking + diverse opponents + impala cnn"; cumulative win rate 91%; weakest matchup Droplet (~55%), strongest vs CoacAI (~95%) — [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2105.13807)

**Invalid-action masking study (Huang & Ontañón, 2020)**
- Proposition: masking is "a state-dependent differentiable function"; gradients for masked actions are zero, so the policy gradient stays valid — [arXiv 2006.14171](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.14171); [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2006.14171)
- Setup: microRTS harvest task on 4×4, 10×10, 16×16, 24×24 maps, PPO, 500,000 steps; masking found the first reward within 0.05–0.08% of training on every map, while the "invalid action penalty" baseline needed 3.43% on 10×10 and degraded further on larger maps; the "naive" variant (masked sampling, unmasked gradient) had the highest return but much higher KL and needed 49.14% of training on 24×24; agents trained with masks and evaluated without them degrade on large maps but still beat penalty-trained agents — [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2006.14171)

**Action guidance: sparse main agent + shaped auxiliary agents (Huang & Ontañón, 2020)**
- Method: a main agent on the sparse reward and auxiliary agents on shaped rewards; with probability ε (0.95 during a "shift" period, then linearly decayed over an "adaptation" period) the environment executes the auxiliary agent's action; both are updated off-policy with PPO clipping; "positive learning optimization" skips updates on zero-reward rollouts — [ar5iv 2010.03956](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2010.03956)
- Budget: 1M timesteps per run, 8 envs × 128 steps, 10 seeds — [ar5iv 2010.03956](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2010.03956)
- DefeatRandomEnemy (sparse ±1; shaped = +5 win, +1 harvest, +1 return, +1 worker, +0.2 building, +1 valid attack, +7 combat unit, +0.2·distance term): sparse-only −0.07 ± 0.03, shaped-only 0.08 ± 0.17, action guidance (long adaptation + PLO) 0.52 ± 0.35 — [ar5iv 2010.03956](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2010.03956)
- ProduceCombatUnits: sparse-only 0.00 ± 0.01, shaped-only 9.57 ± 0.30, guidance (short + PLO) 9.48 ± 0.51; shaped-only agents exhausted harvesting before producing (first unit at t≈1410) while guided agents harvested and produced concurrently (t≈890) — [ar5iv 2010.03956](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2010.03956)
- Negative findings: PLO was "inconclusive" (helped guidance, hurt the sparse baseline, hurt long-adaptation ProduceCombatUnits); short adaptation failed on DefeatRandomEnemy (−0.06 ± 0.04); the shift/adaptation hyperparameters "matter" per task — [ar5iv 2010.03956](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2010.03956)

**RAISocketAI, first DRL winner of the IEEE microRTS competition (Goodfriend, 2024; revised Jan 2025)**
- Scripted agents had won "the five previous iterations"; RAISocketAI's abstract credits "iteratively fine-tuning the base policy and transfer learning to specific maps" and says BC + DRL fine-tuning "has proven promising as an efficient way to bootstrap models" — [arXiv 2402.08112](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.08112)
- Three value heads: (1) shaped reward "similar to MicroRTS-Py except each combat unit type is scaled by build-time", (2) win/loss at game end (tanh head), (3) in-game cost-weighted unit difference. Head weights scheduled [0.8, 0.01, 0.19] → [0, 0.5, 0.5] → [0, 0.99, 0.01] over phases of 90M, 60M, 30M, 60M, 60M steps, ending on "only win-loss sparse rewards" — [arXiv HTML 2402.08112v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.08112v1)
- Behavior cloning from "the 2021 competition winner Mayari playing against itself, 2020 competition winner CoacAI, and POLightRush"; BC loss scaled by 1/(number of units accepting actions), which "allowed the learning rate to be significantly increased" — [arXiv HTML 2402.08112v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.08112v1)
- Opponents: 12 latest-self + 12 old-self environments (50% past selves) in base training; shaped fine-tuning phases used 12 CoacAI/Mayari bot environments and dropped prior-version self-play — [arXiv HTML 2402.08112v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.08112v1)
- Masking: logits of invalid actions set to a very large negative number; extended to treat walls and other units' destinations as invalid move targets — [arXiv HTML 2402.08112v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.08112v1)
- Architecture: DoubleCone(4,6,4) (5,014,865 params, 0.70B MACs on 16×16) and U-Net-like "squnet" (3.58M / 1.42M params for 32/64 maps) — [arXiv HTML 2402.08112v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.08112v1)
- Compute: "70 GPU-days" across 7 networks; base model 12.5 days on an A10; per-map fine-tunes 1.7–5.3 GPU-days; largest map 19.0 GPU-days; 24 envs × 512 rollout steps, minibatch 4096, 2 epochs; 300M base steps + 100M per fine-tune — [arXiv HTML 2402.08112v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.08112v1)
- Measured effects: base model without fine-tuning beat CoacAI in only 20% of games; after three fine-tune iterations 98% (about 90% vs each of CoacAI and Mayari); BC-only (RAI-BC) 71% win rate vs RAI-BC-PPO 88%; transfer learning took BWDistantResources32x32 from below 10% to over 90%; competition result 72% (below baselines 2L at 76% and Mayari at 82%, but it won the competition ranking); the largest map (4)BloodBath.scmB stayed at 25% overall and 0% vs competitive bots, games averaging 3,500 steps vs ~925 on the next map — [arXiv HTML 2402.08112v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.08112v1)
- On why BC helps large maps: imitation provides "non-zero win-loss rewards" early where pure RL would see none — [arXiv HTML 2402.08112v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.08112v1)

**UECD (UCLouvain master's thesis, 2026)**
- U-Net + entity Transformer policy, PPO, "9.47 GPU-days and approximately 350M steps"; 96.67% win rate in a 19-agent tournament; 65.7% head-to-head vs RAISocketAI; ships "85 ablation runs"; contains "a formal analysis of a discount-induced reward collapse under shaped-to-sparse annealing"; uses a two-phase opponent curriculum and prioritized-level-replay over maps; a BC pipeline exists — [GitHub microrts-drl-uecd](https://github.com/mathisdelsart/microrts-drl-uecd)
- The README quotes RAISocketAI at "23.6 GPU-days", which conflicts with the paper's 70 GPU-days for 7 networks (the 23.6 may count only the deployed networks); unresolved — [GitHub microrts-drl-uecd](https://github.com/mathisdelsart/microrts-drl-uecd) vs [arXiv HTML 2402.08112v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.08112v1)

**Competition context**
- The 2025 microRTS competition runs "only a classic track for traditional techniques and an informal track for unproven latest techniques" — [CoG 2025 microRTS competition page](https://cog2025.inesc-id.pt/microrts-competition/)

### Inferences
- In every microRTS success the shaped reward was a bootstrap, never the final objective: Gym-microRTS reports shaped-return/sparse-return divergence, RAISocketAI anneals to 99% win/loss, UECD formally analyses a collapse mode of that annealing. The transferable pattern is "shape early, anneal to outcome, keep an outcome value head throughout".
- RAISocketAI's BC→PPO recipe is the closest published analogue to the Wesnoth project's situation (a cloned seed, then RL). Its BC-only vs BC+PPO gap (71% → 88%) was obtained with 100M-step fine-tunes of 1.7–5.3 A10 GPU-days per map, i.e. tens of dollars per fine-tune at rental prices; that is within the stated budget class, but only for microRTS-size games (~925 steps).
- Gym-microRTS's self-play failure at 300M steps against strong scripted opponents suggests that a fixed pool of competent opponents can be a better teacher than self-play at this budget. Wesnoth's built-in RCA AI is much weaker than CoacAI relative to the field, so this transfer is speculative; the past-self pool (50% old selves) of RAISocketAI is the safer analogue.
- Action guidance's DefeatRandomEnemy result (sparse −0.07, shaped 0.08, guided 0.52 at 1M steps) is the cleanest small-budget evidence that executing a shaped/heuristic policy's actions while training the sparse-objective network beats both alone. With an imitation seed, the seed itself can play the "auxiliary agent" role; that is a speculative transfer.

### Gaps
- Gym-microRTS Appendix C self-play details (why the variants underperformed) were only summarized; the exact numbers were not retrieved.
- The Figure 4 ablation win rates above are approximate readings, not table values.
- RAISocketAI's per-phase ablation of the three reward heads (shaped vs cost-difference vs win/loss) was not found; the paper reports the schedule, not a head-by-head ablation.
- UECD's 85 ablation runs are in the repository's data directory; their findings were not enumerated in the README and were not read.

## Key question 2: Atlatl (NPS) hex wargame RL and other military-simulation wargames: rewards, observations, scripted opponents, curricula

### Takeaway
Atlatl's RL agents learn from a dense per-step score difference (kills, losses, city control), with Boron's engineered variant weighting it by surviving strength plus a 25-point terminal bonus; the sparse-outcome problem is therefore mostly engineered away, and the reported difficulty is scaling. At 10M steps against the scripted Pass-Agg opponent, every single RL model scored below every scripted model (best RL 21.8 vs worst scripted 43.0 over 100,000 games); gains came from switching among scripted and RL models by predicted score (130.5, +62.6% over the best scripted) and from local observation abstraction, while feudal HRL and the full HRL framework did not improve. Outside NPS, potential-based shaping in an NRL capture-the-flag game cut time-to-convergence by 50–87.5% but could cost up to ~20% final score against a hard opponent unless the shaping gradient was retuned, and two AlphaZero-on-hex-wargame efforts (Moy & Shekh 2019; Palma et al. 2025) learned from pure ±1 terminal reward on tiny boards, one by supervised bootstrapping.

### Cited Findings

**Atlatl environment and scoring**
- Atlatl: hex grid, red vs blue alternating turns, units (infantry, mechanized infantry, armor, artillery); "performance is scored based on kills, losses, and holding urban areas"; Gymnasium API with Stable-Baselines 3 hooks — [Black & Darken, I/ITSEC 2023, arXiv 2402.06694](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.06694)
- Score function S_total = S_blue_city + S_blue_combat − (S_red_city + S_red_combat); a unit whose strength drops below 50 is removed and its remaining strength awarded to the opponent — [Black dissertation, NPS June 2024, arXiv 2408.13333](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.13333)
- Scripted opponent Pass-Agg: behavior tree that picks a "passive" or "aggressive" posture from relative combat power, attacks a uniformly chosen in-range enemy if any, otherwise moves by a posture-dependent hex scoring; infantry has range 1 and movement 1 — [arXiv 2408.13333](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.13333)

**Reward designs used**
- Boron reward (eq. 3.6): R_engineered = max(R_raw, 0) · S_c/S_o + B_t·I_t, where R_raw is the score change since the previous step, S_c/S_o current over original friendly strength, B_t "a terminal bonus reward of 25 points that research shows discourages units from moving into the adversary units' attack range during the last turn", I_t the terminal indicator — [arXiv 2408.13333](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.13333)
- Four RL reward variants trained (10M steps each, PPO/DQN via SB3, Pass-Agg opponent, randomly generated scenarios, evaluation every 100,000 steps for 100 episodes): RL-Boron (engineered score reward), RL-Scotty (score-indifferent; rewards staying grouped near cities and massing attacks when superior), RL-S-Killer (reward only for inflicting damage), RL-S-City (reward for occupying cities and staying grouped, attacking only in range) — [arXiv 2408.13333](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.13333)
- Earlier: Boron (2020) trained "a simple neural network" to exhibit mass and economy of force; Cannon & Goericke (2021) added CNNs for spatial invariance across unit and terrain types — [search summary citing NPS theses](https://calhoun.nps.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/d617681f-1bdd-4c72-9479-e3a00cffdf5b/content) (Finley 2023 thesis reference list gives Cannon & Goericke as June 2021, handle 10945/67681)
- Boron (2020) abstract: agents learned the ideal behaviors; "the combat model and reinforcement learning algorithm having the most significant impact"; for massing, training duration and learning rate mattered most; for economy of force, only the discount factor had a significant effect — [Calhoun handle 10945/65414](https://calhoun.nps.edu/handle/10945/65414); conference version Boron & Darken, IEEE CoG 2020, pp. 728–731 — [Crossref 10.1109/CoG47356.2020.9231609](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1109/CoG47356.2020.9231609)

**Results**
- Preliminary (I/ITSEC 2023): 10×10 board, 2–4 agents per side, 1 city, 30 phases, 2M steps on one fixed scenario, 1,000 evaluation games: scripted baseline 813.750 (SD 6.253), RL global observation 1095.601 (SD 62.972), RL local 7×7 observation 1070.512 (SD 24.992); with fully random starting states and 5M steps "the global-observation agent learns very slowly while the three local-observation agents we tested are able to learn relatively quickly" — [arXiv 2402.06694](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.06694)
- Localized observation with piecewise linear spatial decay "consistently outperforms the more traditional global observation approach across increasing scenario complexity levels"; training time "often does so exponentially" with complexity — [Black & Darken 2024, arXiv 2408.13328](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.13328)
- Dissertation Table 4.2 (100,000 games each, mean ± SEM): scripted Pass-Agg 68.962 ± 0.907, Pass 74.964 ± 0.924, Agg 42.995 ± 0.879, Burt-Plus 80.281 ± 0.910; RL-Boron 21.823 ± 0.820, RL-Scotty −11.472 ± 0.803, RL-S-Killer −18.223 ± 0.892, RL-S-City −103.280 ± 0.807; multi-models (score-prediction CNN picks a behavior model per step) Multi(3) 82.812, Multi(4) 93.831, Multi(6) 125.487, Multi(8) 130.540 ± 0.866, "a 62.6% improvement over the best-performing single-model (Burt-Plus)" — [arXiv 2408.13333](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.13333)
- Dissertation Table 5.1 (hybrid: RL for high-level decisions, scripted for low-level; 100,000 games × 5 seeds): overall means scripted −0.904, RL individual −603.371, hybrid 489.998; paired t-tests p < 0.05 on all 10 comparisons — [arXiv 2408.13333](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.13333)
- Abstract: for the full HRL architecture, "initial tests did not show improved performance" — [arXiv 2408.13333](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.13333)
- Rood's feudal multi-agent hierarchy (manager PPO over goal hexes, worker DQN on a 5×5 local view) "failed to demonstrate any learning and underperformed the two other agents" (scripted and flat RL) in five scenarios — [Black dissertation summarizing Rood, arXiv 2408.13333](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.13333)
- Curriculum learning was "not explored in this study" and is proposed as future work — [arXiv 2408.13333](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.13333)
- Hybrid follow-up (Black & Darken, I/ITSEC 2025): scripted agents for tactical decisions, RL for strategic ones; "shown to significantly improve overall performance"; arXiv flags substantial text overlap with 2408.13333 — [arXiv 2512.00249](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.00249)
- NATO STO-MP-MSG-207 paper restates the multi-model and observation-abstraction results — [arXiv 2402.06075](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.06075)

**Other military-simulation wargames**
- NRL, Aquaticus capture-the-flag (Kliem & Dasgupta, 2023): potential-based shaping ρ(s,a,s') = γφ(s') − φ(s) added to the sparse event rewards (tag, grab, capture, out-of-bounds); DQN defender vs scripted attackers Att-E (easy) and Att-H (hard). Tag shaping cut reward convergence from 400K to 200K steps (50%); boundary shaping 75%; both combined 87.5% with performance rising to 11 tags and no self-tags. Against Att-H "the final score of the defender decreases more aggressively by about 20% for different the reward shaping functions" until the shaping gradients were doubled (2×), after which convergence improved "without degrading the scores". Energy-efficiency shaping cost 50–60% of score — [arXiv 2311.16339](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.16339)
- Hex-and-counter wargame with AlphaZero + recurrent "Recall" net and hexagonal convolutions (Palma, Santos, Dias, 2025): reward is terminal only (+1/−1/0); trained on 5×5 boards, 2–4 units, CPU-only, 1,100–3,640 training steps, replay buffer capped at 6,000 games; symmetric scenario >90% vs random and near-perfect vs a Goal-Rush heuristic; curriculum from a pre-trained net converged rapidly; but extrapolation to 6×6–12×12 boards "obtained inferior results to the baseline residual network", attributed to training volume far below AlphaZero's — [arXiv 2502.13918](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.13918)
- AlphaZero on the "Coral Sea" hex wargame (Moy & Shekh, AI 2019): differences from board games are "problem representation, wargame asymmetry, limited strategic depth, and the requirement for significant hardware resources"; "bootstrapping AlphaZero with supervised learning can overcome these challenges", letting it "outperform the supervised examples on which it was trained" — [search summary of Springer chapter 10.1007/978-3-030-35288-2_1](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-030-35288-2_1)
- IDSIA/armasuisse on Command: Modern Operations plus a lightweight twin "war-sim" (del Rio et al., 2025): compared PPO, DQN, AlphaZero on three scenarios; "AlphaZero succeeded in solving all the scenarios" in war-sim, while CMO's instability prevented stable learning; "for complex wargames, tabula rasa learning without any form of human guidance, be it in the form of demonstrations or reward shaping, is currently elusive"; "if no data is available for supervised pre-training reward shaping techniques can be an alternative" — [IDSIA preprint](https://ipg.idsia.ch/preprints/delrio2025a.pdf)
- A 2021 Chinese wargame paper combines intuitionistic-fuzzy multi-attribute weights with actor-critic to build a supplementary reward for "sparse rewards in large-map combat games"; claims only qualitative superiority over pure RL — [arXiv 2109.02354](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.02354)

### Inferences
- Atlatl's per-step score-difference reward is dense (every kill, loss and city change pays out), so its lessons are about credit assignment over 30-phase horizons and scaling, not about sparse outcomes. The Wesnoth analogue of their reward is a per-turn material/village delta, which the project already treats as a training crutch rather than the objective.
- The Atlatl finding that a 10M-step RL agent loses to a two-posture behavior tree, while a learned score predictor that switches among scripted models beats it by 62.6%, is a warning about expecting monolithic RL to beat a decent scripted policy at small budgets, and a hint that value-head-driven selection among fixed policies is cheap and effective.
- Boron's 25-point terminal bonus is a hand-fix for last-turn recklessness under a finite horizon; Wesnoth's capped games raise the same end-of-horizon issue, and a terminal term tied to the final position is the cheap analogue.
- The NRL result quantifies the tradeoff the project should expect from potential-based shaping: faster convergence, with a final-strength cost against strong opponents unless the shaping magnitude is tuned; that tuning needs its own match.
- Two hex-wargame AlphaZero efforts on sparse terminal reward both worked only on tiny boards and one needed supervised bootstrapping; neither is evidence that search-based self-play scales at tens of dollars.

### Gaps
- The Boron (2020) and Cannon & Goericke (2021) theses' full texts were blocked (DTIC and CORE returned 403); reward and hyperparameter details come from abstracts and the Black dissertation's summaries.
- Rood's feudal-hierarchy thesis (DTIC AD1201723) was blocked; only Black's summary is available.
- Moy & Shekh 2019 numbers (win rates, compute) are behind Springer's paywall.
- Atlatl papers report no ablation of reward components (e.g. score-only vs Boron's strength-weighted form).

## Key question 3: Published or community AI/RL work on The Battle for Wesnoth

### Takeaway
Wesnoth-specific learning work is thin and old: a 2012 neural-network recruiter that beat the RCA AI 66.6–69.3% of the time on thousands of games; a 2012–2014 University of Calgary line (Paskaradevan, Wiens, Denzinger) combining RL-learned rule weights with evolved rule sets in a "shout-ahead" unit architecture; and a Gym-like MARL environment (ARLinBfW) with only a qualitative result. No published self-play, search+network, or full-game RL result for Wesnoth was found beyond the user's own repository.

### Cited Findings
- Machine Learning Recruiter (SeattleDad, patch for Wesnoth 1.11.0, v0.3 released 2012-10-25): neural networks from the Waffles toolkit predict per-unit "goodness metrics" from features (enemy/friendly compositions, gold and ratios, terrain coverage, village control, alignment, race, time of day, map size, leader HP); training runs "approximately 600 games in two hours" for new units; the "Recommended" variant raises metric/cost ratios to the 6th power for weighted random selection — [Wesnoth wiki: Machine Learning Recruiter](https://wiki.wesnoth.org/Machine_Learning_Recruiter)
- Results vs RCA AI: ML Recruiter 0.3 (Recommended) 66.6% over 2,363 games; (Less Variety) 69.3% over 1,179 games; Northerners 84.2%; vs Undead it learned to favor impact and fire attacks; later development moved to the AI-Demos GitHub project — [Wesnoth wiki: Machine Learning Recruiter](https://wiki.wesnoth.org/Machine_Learning_Recruiter)
- "Creating large numbers of game AIs by learning behavior for cooperating units" (Wiens, Denzinger, Paskaradevan; IEEE CIG 2013): units decide in two stages (an action without knowledge of others' intentions, then a final decision after exchanging intentions); two rule sets; RL learns rule weights, evolutionary learning evolves rule sets; improvements add terrain knowledge and evaluate on several maps for more general rules — [IEEE Xplore 6633608](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/6633608/) (abstract text via search summary; Crossref confirms venue and authors: [Crossref](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1109/CIG.2013.6633608))
- Related: Paskaradevan & Denzinger, "A Hybrid Cooperative Behavior Learning Method for a Rule-Based Shout-Ahead Architecture" (CIG 2012) and "Learning cooperative behavior for the shout-ahead architecture" (Web Intelligence and Agent Systems, 2014) — [journal PDF, paywalled](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/pdf/10.3233/WIA-140298?download=true); [dblp Paskaradevan](https://dblp.org/pid/130/8326.html)
- ARLinBfW (DStelter94): an OpenAI-Gym-like environment for Wesnoth, Lua add-on streams observations over the console to Python and reads actions from files; run as `wesnoth --multiplayer --nogui --nosound --multiplayer-repeat 1000000 --scenario sce42`; built on Lapan's PTAN code; tests single-agent, multi-agent and "adversarial resilience learning"; reported result: "Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning creates more robust and sometimes also quicker AIs than learning the agent alone" (no numbers in README) — [GitHub ARLinBfW](https://github.com/DStelter94/ARLinBfW)
- GSoC 2014 proposal (Sachith Seneviratne): strategic attack/retreat decisions "by using evaluating functions (or a combination thereof)"; no outcome recorded on the page — [Wesnoth wiki GSoC proposal](https://wiki.wesnoth.org/GSoC_sachith500_Proposal_AI)
- The wiki's Category:AI lists 28 pages (RCA AI, Formula AI, Lua AI, Micro AIs, Experimental AI, etc.); the Machine Learning Recruiter is the only learning-based entry — [Wesnoth wiki Category:AI](https://wiki.wesnoth.org/Category:AI)
- The only self-play RL project found for Wesnoth is the user's own momom2/Wesnoth-AI (BC warm-start from human replays, then self-play) — [GitHub momom2/Wesnoth-AI](https://github.com/momom2/Wesnoth-AI)

### Inferences
- The ML Recruiter's 66–69% over RCA came from a learned per-unit value used inside a fixed rule structure, on hundreds of cheap games; it is the one Wesnoth-native precedent that a narrow learned component measured against the built-in AI with thousands of games is affordable and detectable.
- Nothing in the Wesnoth literature addresses sparse win/loss learning for the full game; the project's prior evidence base for that has to come from the other platforms in this note.

### Gaps
- The shout-ahead papers' numbers (fitness gains, win rates vs the built-in AI, maps) are behind IEEE/Sage paywalls; the Semantic Scholar API rate-limited and Crossref carries no abstract.
- The Wesnoth forum thread "Programmable AI that learns from a user" (t=30862) is behind an anti-bot wall; not read.
- No Wesnoth MCTS/minimax or neural-search thesis surfaced in two searches; absence of evidence, not evidence of absence.

## Key question 4: CivRealm/Freeciv, Lux AI, Advance Wars, Polytopia, tactical-RPG RL: techniques with measured gains on sparse, long-horizon, multi-unit turn-based reward

### Takeaway
The one measured, staged ablation in this group is Lux AI Season 1 (Chen et al. 2023): pure win/loss from scratch gave 0% win rate, dense shaping alone 50%, adding an end-of-game scaled margin reward ±√|N_self − N_opp| 85%, and finishing on ±1 win/loss above 90% (600 CPU cores + one V100, ~5M episodes in phase 1). The Kaggle winner used a similar shape-then-sparse schedule plus a frozen-teacher KL term to stop strategic cycling, on a personal dual-GPU PC. CivRealm's PPO baseline on the sparse civilization score stayed myopic after 2M+ steps; Polytopia (Tribes) has only forward-model search results, and the Fire Emblem paper used a small mixed reward with BC+GAIL+PPO at 1M steps. No Advance Wars RL work was found.

### Cited Findings

**CivRealm / Freeciv**
- CivRealm (Qi et al., ICLR 2024): the full-game reward is the civilization score aggregating "population, researched technologies, wonders, units built and killed, culture, and if spaceship criteria is achieved"; "Rewards are sparse, delayed, and asynchronous"; mini-games have stepwise rewards plus binary victory; the tensor baseline is PPO with an AlphaStar-style hierarchy (MLP/Transformer/CNN encoders, RNN memory, an actor head choosing unit/city/government/tech/end-turn then a pointer network over action ids), run on Ray — [arXiv 2401.10568](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.10568); [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.10568)
- Results: mini-game CityTileWonder ~90% success, "shortcut learning" on hard levels; full game after 2M+ steps shows rising score but "myopic" unit-production strategy without founding cities; state-action space grows from 10^15 to 10^650; conclusion: RL does well "for tasks offering immediate rewards and requiring relatively short-term planning" and struggles with "sparse and delayed rewards, requiring long-term strategic planning" — [arXiv HTML 2401.10568](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.10568)
- Knowledge-based RL for Freeciv (Voss et al., 2019): ~440 common rules plus 11 expert rule sets (523–755 rules each); RL (Monte Carlo over a 185-cluster abstract state from 33 features) resolves conflicts among applicable rules; reward minimizes turns to victory; 550 games; trained agent wins in 287 turns on average vs 291 for the best expert set and 100% vs the expert knowledge bases — [ar5iv 1908.05472](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1908.05472)
- Ulam, Goel et al. (AIIDE): model-based meta-reasoning plus RL on a Freeciv "defend a city" task learned to expert-designed-agent level faster than pure RL — [AIIDE proceedings](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AIIDE/article/view/18685)

**Lux AI Season 1 (Kaggle 2021)**
- Toad Brigade (1st place) RL component: reward shaping "for the first 20 million steps" for building/losing cities and units, researching, fueling cities, plus win/loss; then sparse win/loss; an 8-block net on shaped reward, then 16- and 24-block nets on sparse reward "with the smaller previous networks as teachers"; a frozen teacher's policy enters as a KL loss "to stabilize behavior and prevent strategic cycles"; IMPALA (FAIR implementation) + UPGO + TD-λ; illegal actions masked by setting logits to −∞; 24-block SE-ResNet, ~20M params; trained "overnight most nights" on "an 8-core/16-thread dual-GPU" personal PC; no formal ablations, but a "game phase" input feature produced "dramatically different behaviors" by phase — [Toad Brigade README](https://github.com/IsaiahPressman/Kaggle_Lux_AI_2021/blob/main/README.md)
- Chen et al. (Parametrix, 2023) three-phase curriculum: Phase 1 dense (0.01 per research point, 0.5 per unit built, 0.0001 per fuel, 1.0 per CityTile); Phase 2 end-of-episode ±√|N_self − N_op| in city tiles; Phase 3 ±1 win/loss; PPO + GAE, pixel-to-pixel ResNet emitting an action map for all units; win rate 50% after phase 1, 85% after phase 2, "above 90%" after phase 3; "directly training with sparse reward" gave 0%; compute "600 CPU cores" and one V100; phase 1 converged around 5M episodes — [ar5iv 2301.01609](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2301.01609)

**Lux AI Season 2 and 3**
- Season 2 (NeurIPS 2023) was won by a rules-based agent — [ryandy/Lux-S2-public](https://github.com/ryandy/Lux-S2-public); the top DRL entry (FLG, 4th) used a "DoubleCone" backbone with middle blocks downscaled 4× for inference speed — [FLG Kaggle writeup (title only readable)](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/lux-ai-season-2/writeups/flg-flg-s-approach-deep-reinforcement-learning-wit); described in [RAISocketAI, arXiv HTML 2402.08112v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.08112v1)
- Season 3 (NeurIPS 2024) added partial observability and a 5-game meta-learning format with a JAX GPU environment; no winner writeup found — [NeurIPS competition page](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2024/competition/84798)

**Polytopia**
- Tribes (Perez-Liebana et al., AIIDE 2020): forward-model framework; agents are rule-based, one-step lookahead, MC, MCTS, RHEA with a 7-feature heuristic; RHEA beats MCTS 63% and the rule-based agent 58.6%, the rule-based agent ranks second; no RL agent — [AIIDE 2020 PDF](https://cdn.aaai.org/ojs/7438/7438-52-10764-1-2-20200923.pdf)
- tribes-rl (Pham Tuan Binh, C reimplementation for PufferLib): shaped reward with terminal win bonus (1 + speed_bonus·(1 − turns/200)), draw −0.5 at the 200-turn cap, +0.05 per city, +0.3 per capital, 0.01 per star of unit trades, 0.001 per tile revealed, 0.01 per income point, −0.003 per turn, −0.02 per star burned; one masked Discrete(197) head through a three-phase state machine; 8.2M-parameter MinGRU; 4096 parallel agents at "1.8M agent steps per second" on an RTX 5090, 10B steps in under 2 hours; no win rates, Elo or ablations published — [GitHub tribes-rl](https://github.com/pham-tuan-binh/tribes-rl)
- DeepMind integrated Polytopia into AndroidEnv as a task for "long term planning, imperfect information, diverse UI elements, and non-determinism"; no results — [PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/deepmind-ai-battle-polytopia-androidenv/)

**Fire Emblem-like tactics**
- "Mirror Mode in Fire Emblem" (Smid, van der Putten, Plaat, 2025): simplified Fire Emblem Heroes in Unity, 6×8 grid, 4 units per side; reward win +1, unit death −1, valid action +0.3, loss or exceeding a 20-action cap −1; invalid actions masked; BC pre-training, then PPO + GAIL + BC + extrinsic reward (strength 0.5); 10 environments (4 axis-flip augmentations); 200K steps for tuning, 1M for user-study agents; imitation matched defensive/retreat behavior but "agents rarely perform attacks"; PPO alone reached the highest cumulative reward (~0.2) — [arXiv 2512.11902](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.11902); [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.11902)

**Advance Wars**
- Three searches (AWBW bots, Advance Wars RL agents, PPO on Advance Wars clones) returned no RL research or measured agent; only notifier bots and web reimplementations — [GitHub topic advance-wars](https://github.com/topics/advance-wars)

### Inferences
- The Lux S1 curriculum is the only multi-unit turn-based result with a per-stage number: the intermediate "scaled sparse" stage (±√margin at game end) was worth +35 points of win rate on top of dense shaping, and dense shaping was worth +50 over nothing. Its analogue for Wesnoth is a terminal reward proportional to a signed function of final material or village margin, applied especially to capped games where the ±1 outcome is undefined; that is a change to the terminal label only, so existing game records can be relabelled without new games (speculative transfer, but the cost claim follows from the design).
- Toad Brigade's frozen-teacher KL is a direct analogue of keeping the imitation seed as a regularizer during self-play; both Lux winners and RAISocketAI kept a strong prior policy in the loss rather than trusting the outcome signal alone.
- CivRealm's failure mode (score rises, strategy myopic) is what to expect from optimizing a dense proxy with a small budget; matches against a fixed reference, as the project already requires, are the guard.
- Fire Emblem's +0.3 per valid action is a crude "act more" bonus; the project's end_turn logit offset produced +193–229 Elo for the same behavioral lever without training, so this is corroboration that "act more" is a known lever, not evidence for the reward form.

### Gaps
- Chen et al.'s win rates (50/85/>90%) are against an opponent the summary did not name; the exact evaluation protocol was not retrieved.
- FLG's Lux S2 reward curriculum details are on a JS-rendered Kaggle page that could not be read.
- Lux S3 (2024–25) winner and methods not found.
- No RL work on Advance Wars or AWBW was found.
- tribes-rl publishes no win rates, so its shaping weights are a design record, not evidence.

## Key question 5: Which techniques are cheap to test (no new games or a few hundred) and which need large runs

### Takeaway
Cheap, on the evidence above: relabelling terminal outcomes (scaled margin at the cap, terminal bonus), adding a KL-to-frozen-teacher term, value-head-driven selection among fixed policies, and any decode-time rule; each changes only the loss or the decode and is verified by one match. Medium: BC→PPO fine-tuning on the existing seed with a past-self opponent pool (RAISocketAI's 100M-step fine-tunes at 1.7–5.3 A10 GPU-days each). Expensive and with the worst track record at small budgets: dense-shaping-from-scratch curricula (Lux: 600 CPU cores, ~5M episodes per phase), full HRL/feudal decompositions (Atlatl: no gain or failure to learn), and AlphaZero-style self-play on sparse reward at board sizes beyond toy (Palma: extrapolation failed; RAISocketAI: 0% on the largest map after 19 GPU-days).

### Cited Findings
- Compute anchors: Gym-microRTS 300M steps, 37–117 h on one g4dn.xlarge — [ar5iv 2105.13807](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2105.13807); RAISocketAI 70 GPU-days across 7 networks, 100M-step fine-tunes at 1.7–5.3 GPU-days on an A10 — [arXiv HTML 2402.08112v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.08112v1); UECD 9.47 GPU-days, ~350M steps — [GitHub microrts-drl-uecd](https://github.com/mathisdelsart/microrts-drl-uecd); Lux S1 curriculum 600 CPU cores + V100, ~5M episodes for phase 1 — [ar5iv 2301.01609](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2301.01609); Toad Brigade: personal dual-GPU PC, overnight runs — [Toad Brigade README](https://github.com/IsaiahPressman/Kaggle_Lux_AI_2021/blob/main/README.md); Atlatl 10M steps per model, SB3 on Gymnasium — [arXiv 2408.13333](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.13333); Action guidance 1M steps, 8 envs — [ar5iv 2010.03956](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2010.03956); Palma et al. CPU-only, 1,100–3,640 training steps — [arXiv 2502.13918](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.13918); NRL CTF 200K–800K DQN steps — [arXiv 2311.16339](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.16339)
- Techniques that changed only the loss or the label and reported gains: frozen-teacher KL "to stabilize behavior and prevent strategic cycles" — [Toad Brigade README](https://github.com/IsaiahPressman/Kaggle_Lux_AI_2021/blob/main/README.md); end-of-episode ±√|margin| stage worth 50% → 85% win rate — [ar5iv 2301.01609](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2301.01609); Boron's 25-point terminal bonus against last-turn recklessness — [arXiv 2408.13333](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.13333); BC loss scaled by 1/(units acting) allowing a much higher learning rate — [arXiv HTML 2402.08112v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.08112v1)
- Techniques that changed only the decode or the policy-selection layer: score-prediction CNN selecting among 8 fixed models, +62.6% over the best single model in 100,000 games — [arXiv 2408.13333](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.13333); action guidance executing an auxiliary policy's actions with ε = 0.95 while the sparse-objective net trains — [ar5iv 2010.03956](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2010.03956)
- Techniques that needed scale or failed: Gym-microRTS self-play variants underperformed scripted-opponent training at 300M steps — [ar5iv 2105.13807](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2105.13807); Atlatl HRL "did not show improved performance", feudal hierarchy "failed to demonstrate any learning" — [arXiv 2408.13333](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.13333); RAISocketAI largest map 0% vs competitive bots after 19 GPU-days — [arXiv HTML 2402.08112v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.08112v1); CivRealm full game myopic after 2M+ steps — [arXiv HTML 2401.10568](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.10568); Palma et al. recurrent net worse than residual baseline on larger boards — [arXiv 2502.13918](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.13918)
- Shaping's cost: NRL found up to ~20% lower final score against the hard attacker until shaping gradients were retuned — [arXiv 2311.16339](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.16339); Gym-microRTS saw shaped return rise with high-variance sparse return — [ar5iv 2105.13807](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2105.13807)

### Inferences
- Cheap (one 800-game match each, no new generation needed to implement): (1) KL-to-seed regularization on any self-play or fine-tune leg; (2) terminal-label relabelling for capped games with a bounded signed margin (Lux phase 2 form), which can be applied retroactively to stored records; (3) a terminal bonus/penalty for end-state exposure (Boron form); (4) value-head gated switching between the reference decode and a second fixed policy (Atlatl multi-model form), which needs only the existing value head and a handful of candidate policies. Each is a one-factor change with a natural kill criterion.
- Medium (one to a few GPU-days, i.e. tens of dollars at 4090 rental rates): RAISocketAI-style PPO fine-tune from the seed with a 50% past-self opponent pool and a reward schedule that starts dense and ends on the outcome head. The published analogue's gain (71% → 88%) was on ~925-step games; Wesnoth games at the cap are long and the actor count, not the trainer, was the project's binding constraint, so the cost estimate should come from the project's own leaves-per-second numbers, not from the microRTS figures.
- Expensive or unpromising at this budget: from-scratch dense-shaping curricula (Lux-scale), hierarchical/feudal decompositions (no Atlatl gain), and AlphaZero-style sparse self-play on full maps (the two hex-wargame attempts worked only on tiny boards, and the project's own 2026-09-04 review found search-32 below argmax).
- Two negative transfers to flag: Gym-microRTS's scripted-opponent pool beat self-play because CoacAI was near the top of the field, whereas Wesnoth's RCA AI is weak relative to the imitation seed, so a scripted pool is unlikely to be a useful teacher here; and shaped rewards in every source above were either annealed away or shown to cost final strength, so a shaped leg without an outcome-only phase and an 800-game match is not supported by any result found.

### Gaps
- No source measured the same technique on the same game with and without an imitation seed; the "cheap" ranking above is inferred from what each technique touches (label, loss, decode) and from the reported compute, not from a direct cost study.
- No source reports the effect of relabelling capped/undecided games; Lux's ±√margin is the nearest evidence and its evaluation opponent is unknown.
- No affordable-compute source reports a positive result for self-play alone on a sparse two-player turn-based wargame.
