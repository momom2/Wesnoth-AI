# Hierarchical, modular and neuro-symbolic architectures for strategy-game agents

Scope: systems where strategic decisions are learned (or searched) over macro-actions, intents or scripts, and tactical execution is delegated to algorithmic experts, scripts or sub-policies. Primary sources were read in full where the PDF could be extracted (Cicero TR, piKL, HMS, JueWu-SL, MGG, TStarBots, Pang et al., OpenAI Five, PGS, POE, SSS, Puppet Search, Mariño 2019/2021, Prismata HPS, Online Evolution, DDO, FeUdal, AI-and-Wargaming); the rest are cited from abstracts or HTML renderings and marked as such. Years are given per source.

## Q1. Which systems learned a policy over macro-actions with experts underneath, and what did they report about sample efficiency, credit assignment or strength versus monolithic end-to-end agents?

### Takeaway
Every strong deployed system in this family (Cicero/Diplodocus, Tencent HMS and JueWu-SL, TStarBot1, Pang et al.'s SC2 agent, Prismata's HPS, MicroRTS competition winners) puts hand-written or imitation-derived structure under a learned or searched top level, and the direct ablations that exist (HMS 75-25 with vs without macro layer; Pang et al. hierarchy vs single policy at level 7; Zhang et al. 89% vs 62%; Barriga et al. combined > either component) favour the decomposition, but none of them is an equal-compute comparison against a monolithic agent, and the two monolithic giants (OpenAI Five, AlphaStar) reached superhuman play without a learned hierarchy while still delegating some decisions to scripts or human-derived statistics.

### Cited Findings

**Cicero (Diplomacy, Meta FAIR, Science, 9 Dec 2022)**
- Architecture: a dialogue model controllable through "intents", defined as "a set of planned actions for the agent and its speaking partner", and a strategic reasoning module that "runs a planning algorithm that predicts the policies of all other players ... and chooses an optimal action for Cicero"; the planner "relies on a value and policy function trained through self-play RL that penalized the agent for deviating too far from human behavior". — [Cicero TR (Science 2022)](https://noambrown.com/papers/22-Science-Diplomacy-TR.pdf); [Science DOI](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.ade9097)
- Why not pure self-play: "Even in dialogue-free versions of Diplomacy, we found that a self-play algorithm that achieved superhuman performance in 2p0s versions of the game performed poorly in games with multiple human players owing to learning a policy inconsistent with the norms and expectations of potential human allies". — [Cicero TR](https://noambrown.com/papers/22-Science-Diplomacy-TR.pdf)
- Value training: self-play "operated similarly to AlphaZero and ReBeL by applying planning 'in the loop' as the improvement operator", using CoShar piKL with "the anchor policy ... fixed throughout training to anchor the RL near human play". — [Cicero TR](https://noambrown.com/papers/22-Science-Diplomacy-TR.pdf)
- Results: 40 anonymous blitz-league games, "ranked in the top 10% of participants who played more than one game and second out of 19 participants in the league that played five or more games"; mean score 25.8% vs 12.4% average of its 82 opponents; first in an 8-game, 21-participant tournament. Training data: 125,261 games. — [Cicero TR](https://noambrown.com/papers/22-Science-Diplomacy-TR.pdf)
- Stated limits: "Cicero reasoned about dialogue purely in terms of players' actions for the current turn. It did not model how its dialogue might affect the relationship with other players over the long-term course of a game"; "the expressive power of our intent representation limited Cicero's ability to control richer affordances of dialogue". — [Cicero TR](https://noambrown.com/papers/22-Science-Diplomacy-TR.pdf)

**piKL and Diplodocus (the strategic layer, quantified)**
- piKL (Jacob et al., arXiv Dec 2021): KL-regularized search toward an imitation anchor. No-press Diplomacy: the IL policy scores 7.9% ± 0.4% against a population; piKL-HedgeBot at λ = 10^-3 scores 32.9% ± 0.7% (RMBot, unregularized, 31.3%; SearchBot 13.4%). Human top-1 move prediction: IL ~43%, piKL λ=10^-1 ~43% while scoring ~16.5% vs the IL's ~7.9%; λ=10^-3 ~41% accuracy; unregularized Hedge/RM ~37%. Chess (Maia1900): 53.2% raw → 54.3% with MCTS at c_puct=2; Go: 57.8% → 58.5%. — [piKL, arXiv 2112.07544](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.07544) (numbers read from the ar5iv rendering)
- Diplodocus (Bakhtin et al., arXiv Oct 2022, RL-DiL-piKL): 200-game human tournament, 62 participants: Diplodocus-High ranked 1st (27% ± 4% average score, 50 games), Diplodocus-Low 3rd (26% ± 4%), best human 2nd (25% ± 6%, 13 games), BRBot (best response to BC) 6th (23% ± 4%), DORA (pure self-play) 32nd (13% ± 3%). Population evaluation over 1,000 games: Diplodocus-Low 29% ± 1%, DORA 13% ± 1%. "DORA achieved superhuman performance in a 2p0s version of Diplomacy without human data, but in the full 7-player game plays poorly with agents other than itself." — [Diplodocus, arXiv 2210.05492](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.05492)

**Tencent Hierarchical Macro Strategy (HMS), Honor of Kings (Wu et al., arXiv Dec 2018, AAAI 2019)**
- Supervised two-layer macro model (phase layer as auxiliary task, attention layer predicting the region to go to) whose "output of attention layer directly conveys macro strategy embedding to micro level models"; the micro network is "almost identical to the one used in OpenAI Five ... but in a supervised learning manner". — [HMS, arXiv 1812.07887](https://arxiv.org/abs/1812.07887)
- Motivation stated by the authors: "reasoning macro strategy implicitly by learning upon micro level action space may be too difficult. OpenAI Five's ability gap between micro level execution and macro strategy operation was obvious." — [HMS](https://arxiv.org/abs/1812.07887)
- Results (Table 2): 48.3% win rate over 250 games against human teams ranked in the top 1% (King); HMS vs "AI Without Macro Strategy" 75%-25% over 40 games (the ablated AI "mainly focused on nearby targets"); vs "Without Communication" 62.5%-37.5%; vs "Without Phase Layer" 65%-35%. Against humans the AI "did not have advantage on teamfight" (15% fewer kills) but "destroyed 2.5 more turrets than human on average in the first 10 minutes". — [HMS](https://arxiv.org/abs/1812.07887)
- Cost: ~300k professional-league replays → 250M training instances; 8 GPUs, ~12 hours (Caffe). — [HMS](https://arxiv.org/abs/1812.07887)

**JueWu-SL, Honor of Kings (Ye et al., IEEE TNNLS 2020)**
- Macro-strategy (global and local intent) trained as an auxiliary task whose softmax outputs feed the micro action heads, all in one supervised network; tested "competitively at the level of High King players in standard 5v5 games". — [JueWu-SL, arXiv 2011.12582](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.12582)
- Direct comparisons (100 matches each): JueWu-SL beats a behaviour tree 100%, HMS (macro output embedded into the same micro model) 86%, and a "two-stage" macro-then-micro pipeline 92%. Ablation: without the macro-intent auxiliary task the win rate against the full model drops to 31%; without vector features 17%; without scene-based sampling 21%. — [JueWu-SL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.12582)

**Macro-Goals Guided RL (MGG), Honor of Kings (Gao et al., NeurIPS 2021)**
- A supervised Meta-Controller predicts human macro-goals; the RL policy is conditioned on the goal and gets an intrinsic reward equal to the decrease in distance to the goal, "which enhances policy diversity" and "avoid[s] the decline of micro-operation ability caused by completely imitation learning". — [MGG, NeurIPS 2021](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2021/file/86dba86754c0ad93997a11fa947d97b2-Paper.pdf)
- Results over 102 heroes: Elo MGG 3048 vs RL-baseline 2851 vs SL agent 2183 vs average human 1788; win rates against humans 97.81% (MGG), 97.77% (RL-baseline), 93.10% (SL); in two special lineups MGG gains +312 and +560 Elo over the RL baseline; macro-state entropy 0.408 vs 0.014 for the RL baseline. — [MGG](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2021/file/86dba86754c0ad93997a11fa947d97b2-Paper.pdf)
- Cost: Meta-Controller 8 P40 GPUs for ~26 h; RL on "60,000 CPUs and 830 NVIDIA V100 GPUs", 420 hours, "the same computation resources as the original actor-critic framework". — [MGG](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2021/file/86dba86754c0ad93997a11fa947d97b2-Paper.pdf)

**Zhang et al., "Hierarchical RL for Multi-agent MOBA Game" (arXiv Jan 2019)**
- Imitation-learned macro level over four action types masks the PPO micro policy's action probabilities; 1v1 vs built-in AI: HRL 89%/83%/80% (entry/easy/medium) vs flat PPO 62%/60%/55%; training "seven days for agents on one Tesla P40 GPU". Labelling procedure sparsely described; no human baseline. — [arXiv 1901.08004](https://arxiv.org/abs/1901.08004) (from the ar5iv rendering)

**TStarBots, StarCraft II full game (Sun et al., Tencent, arXiv Sep 2018)**
- TStarBot1: "a single global controller, which will be learned by RL and it makes decisions over macro actions ... At the bottom, there is a pool of macro actions, which hard-codes prior knowledge of game rules (e.g. TechTree)". 165 macro actions; one macro action every 32 frames (~2 s), "which shortens the time horizon to about 300 ∼ 1200 steps per game". — [TStarBots, arXiv 1809.07193](https://arxiv.org/abs/1809.07193)
- Results: after "1 ∼ 2 days of training with a single GPU and 3840 CPUs" (1920 actors, ~16,000 frames/s), DDQN and PPO agents "win more than 90% of games against all built-in bots from level-1 to level-9, and more than 70% against level-10" (200 games each, fog on). Sample milestones: beats level-2 at ~30M frames (~0.06M games), level-4 at ~250M (~0.5M games), level-6 at ~800M (~1.6M games), level-10 at ~3500M frames (~7M games). — [TStarBots](https://arxiv.org/abs/1809.07193)
- TStarBot2 (hierarchical macro-micro action set with rule-based controllers) "is able to consistently defeat built-in AIs in all levels"; the authors did not learn its hierarchy: "learning methods for the complex hierarchical RL will be needed. To avoid the difficulty, we simply adopt a rule based controller". Head to head, "TStarBot1 can always defeat TStarBot2" via Zergling rush; vs humans both bots went 1/2 vs Platinum and 0/3, 0/2 vs Diamond 2-3, and "TStarBot1 will lose consistently once a human player figures out TStarBot1's preference for Zergling Rush". — [TStarBots](https://arxiv.org/abs/1809.07193)

**Pang et al., "On RL for Full-length Game of StarCraft" (Nanjing, AAAI 2019)**
- Two-layer hierarchy: a controller picks a sub-policy every 8 s, sub-policies (base network, battle policy) pick a macro-action every 1 s; the controller's reward is the sum of the sub-policy's rewards over the interval. Trained on "a single machine with 4 GPUs and 48 CPU threads" (50 parallel SC2 environments, 100 games per iteration ≈ 4 min); 64×64 map, two unit types only. — [Pang et al., arXiv 1809.09095](https://arxiv.org/abs/1809.09095)
- Results: mixture model wins 100/100/99/97/100/90/93/74/71/43% vs built-in levels 1-10 (100 games each). Hierarchy vs single policy: "on low difficulty levels, the single-policy architecture can be almost as good as hierarchical architecture ... when moving to high level of difficulty, the final performance of the hierarchical architecture are significantly better" (Fig. 4a, level 7). Win/loss reward alone "is relatively poor" at level 7 versus handcrafted dense rewards. — [Pang et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1809.09095)

**OpenAI Five (Berner et al., arXiv Dec 2019), the monolithic reference**
- "on an average timestep our model chooses among 8,000 to 80,000 actions (depending on hero)"; "Certain game mechanics were controlled by hand-scripted logic rather than the policy: the order in which heroes purchase items and abilities, control of the unique courier unit, and which items heroes keep in reserve. While we believe the agent could ultimately perform better if these actions were not scripted, we achieved superhuman performance before doing so." — [OpenAI Five, arXiv 1912.06680](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.06680)
- Credit assignment without hierarchy: base horizon 180 s (γ = 0.9993); "resuming training a skilled agent using a longer horizon makes it perform better, up to the longest horizons we explored (6-12 minutes)". Compute: "770±50 PFlops/s·days"; Rerun "2 months and 150 ± 5 PFlops/s·days"; up to 1,536 GPUs at peak. — [OpenAI Five](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.06680)
- On AlphaStar: "AlphaStar handled similar decisions (e.g. building order) by conditioning (during training) on statistics derived from human replays." — [OpenAI Five](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.06680)

**Learned build-order selection over scripts, StarCraft: Brood War (Gehring et al., FAIR, arXiv Nov 2018)**
- CherryPi's 25 rule-based build orders (42 race-specific actions); a 2048-cell LSTM predicts the win probability of each build order from partial observations, decision every 5 s of game time, argmax with a 0.01 switching threshold; trained on 2.8M generated games with 3.3M random switching points. Win rate vs training bots 0.878-0.888 against a 0.793 fixed-strategy baseline; vs held-out bots 0.635 (Locutus) and 0.706 (McRave); "gains for variants with memory observations on the training bots can not be transferred to held-out bots". — [arXiv 1811.08568](https://arxiv.org/abs/1811.08568) (ar5iv rendering)

**Hand-coded arbitration: UAlbertaBot and Wesnoth's RCA AI**
- UAlbertaBot: StrategyManager gives build-order goals to a ProductionManager that runs BOSS search; "All battles UAlbertaBot encounters are first run through our SparCraft combat simulation system. If the outcome of this simulation is positive we continue toward the enemy base. If the outcome is negative, we retreat"; "UAlbertaBot can use the UCB algorithm for selecting a strategy to use against a given opponent, based on the results of previous matches" (disabled by default as of 2013); unit micro is "rule-based scripted policy". — [UAlbertaBot wiki](https://github.com/davechurchill/ualbertabot/wiki/Artificial-Intelligence)
- Wesnoth RCA AI (default since 1.9): candidate actions with fixed scores evaluated each step and "the CA with the highest evaluation score is executed and the process is repeated, until no more valid moves are found": goto 200,000; retreat injured 192,000/95,000 (Lua, 1.15.3+); spread poison 190,000; recruitment 180,000; move leader to goals 140,000; move leader to keep 120,000; high-XP attack 100,010; combat 100,000; place healers 96,000; healing 80,000; villages 60,000; retreat 40,000 (removed in 1.15.3+); move to targets 20,000; leader shares keep 10,000; move to any enemy 1,000. Aspects (aggression default 0.4, caution, leader_value 3, village_value 1) parametrize the CAs; "targets apply to the move-to-targets CA only"; C++ CAs have fixed scores, Lua CAs may score dynamically. — [Wesnoth wiki: RCA AI](https://wiki.wesnoth.org/RCA_AI)
- A learned module inside that structure: the "Machine Learning Recruiter" (SeattleDad, Wesnoth 1.11.0, 2012) replaced the recruitment CA with a Waffles neural net over ~30 game-state features, trained on thousands of games against the RCA AI (1,179 and 2,363 games cited), and reported 66.6% and 69.3% win rates against the RCA recruiter across the default 2p factions; it was not merged and moved to the AI-Demos project. — [Wesnoth wiki: Machine Learning Recruiter](https://wiki.wesnoth.org/Machine_Learning_Recruiter)

**Learned high level, script/search low level, in μRTS and micro**
- Barriga, Stanescu, Buro (arXiv Sep 2017): a CNN "trained by supervised learning on game states labelled by Puppet Search" selects an abstract action for all units, then "the game tree search algorithm improves the tactical actions of a subset of units using a limited view of the game state only considering units close to opponent units"; the combination "achieved higher win-rates than either of its two independent components and other state-of-the-art μRTS agents" (abstract only; numbers not retrieved). — [arXiv 1709.03480](https://arxiv.org/abs/1709.03480)
- HRL-IM/CBS (arXiv 2026): a hierarchy of tabular Q-tables over influence-map hashes selecting among 12 hand-written cluster-based scripts for StarCraft micro (4v4/8v8 Marines); versus MAPPO it reaches higher final scores in two scenarios (47.8 vs 20.8; 77.2 vs 65.4) and a lower test win rate in the hardest (0.925 vs 1.0); the authors attribute the faster rise to "high-quality sampling driven by cluster-based scripts from early training" and note robustness "remains limited" in complex settings; the training budget is not stated. — [arXiv 2606.30092](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.30092)
- Wargaming HRL (arXiv Aug 2024): a hybrid "leveraging RL for high-level decisions and scripted agents for lower-level tasks"; the abstract reports that "initial tests did not show improved performance". — [arXiv 2408.13333](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.13333)

**Survey view**
- Goodman, Risi, Lucas, "AI and Wargaming" (arXiv Sep 2020): script-based action abstraction lets "the AI focus on learning which tactic to apply in a given situation without needing to learn the detailed instructions that make it up ... The net result is to significantly speed up the learning or planning process (by orders of magnitude) at a cost in loss of flexibility"; "The MicroRTS winners tend to use a two-level hierarchical approach to select amongst scripts to use for different units"; competition winners "use planning and search-based methods with little direct RL, even though the decision time for each move is only in the range 40-100 milliseconds on a single processor." — [arXiv 2009.08922](https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.08922)

### Inferences
- The consistent pattern is that the top level is either imitation-trained (HMS, JueWu-SL, MGG's meta-controller, Cicero's anchor) or a search with an exact forward model (piKL, Puppet Search, HPS); nobody reports a strong strategy-game agent whose top level was learned from sparse outcomes alone over expert sub-modules, except TStarBot1 and Pang et al. against scripted built-in AIs with dense shaped rewards.
- Where an ablation exists, the gain from the explicit macro layer is large in supervised settings (HMS 75-25; JueWu-SL 69-31) and modest-to-nil in RL settings once the RL baseline is already strong (MGG: 97.81% vs 97.77% against humans; Elo +197 mostly from lineup-specific play).
- The HMS and JueWu-SL numbers are "with macro layer" vs "same micro model without it" under equal data; they are not comparisons against an end-to-end RL agent of equal compute, so they do not settle the hierarchy-vs-monolith question.

### Gaps
- No source gives an equal-compute comparison of a learned-macro/expert-micro agent against a monolithic agent in a turn-based wargame. Pang et al.'s Fig. 4a is the closest (hierarchy vs single policy over macro-actions), and it is against the scripted built-in AI.
- Cicero's and Diplodocus's RL compute budgets were not found in the text read.
- The claim (from a tool summary of the OpenAI Five PDF) that the authors "explicitly chose against hierarchical RL" could not be verified in the paper text; the only hierarchical-RL mention found is a bibliography entry. Treat as unverified.
- Barriga et al. 2017 numbers (win rates of CNN+search vs each component) were not retrieved.

## Q2. How were macro-action spaces designed and how large were they? Did anyone learn macro-actions or intents from human demonstrations, and how was the labelling done?

### Takeaway
Reported macro spaces are small (4-8 script choices in Puppet Search, 12 partial players in Prismata, 42 build orders in CherryPi, 165 macro actions in TStarBot1, 144-576 map regions in HMS/JueWu-SL) and were hand-designed from game structure; the human-derived labels that worked at scale are spatial "where the next attacks happen" targets (HMS, JueWu-SL) and future macro-state at a fixed horizon (MGG), plus Cicero's action-set intents produced by a dialogue-conditional action predictor; sequence-mining (Pang et al.) and option-discovery methods (DDO, CompILE, Option-GAIL) have only been demonstrated on Atari-RAM, grid worlds and MuJoCo.

### Cited Findings

**Hand-designed macro spaces**
- TStarBot1: 165 macro actions for Zerg-vs-Zerg: 13 building, 22 unit production, 27 tech upgrades, 3 resource-collection, and 100 combat actions from 10 map zones ("combat units in Zone-X start to attack Zone-Y if there are enemies there, otherwise, rally to Zone-Y and wait"), with "hit-and-run" micro hard-coded; a TechTree-derived available-action list masks invalid macro actions. In the reported experiments combat actions were pruned to three. — [TStarBots](https://arxiv.org/abs/1809.07193)
- Puppet Search: "PuppetSingle" exposes one choice point among 4 rush scripts (branching factor 4); "PuppetBasic" adds an expand-or-not choice (branching 4 or 8). Scripts are "a function that takes a game state and returns a set of actions to be performed now ... The only requirement is that the method must be able to generate actions for any legal game state." — [Puppet Search, TCIAIG 2017](https://skatgame.net/mburo/ps/TCIAIG17-puppet.pdf)
- Prismata HPS: Master bot "Uses a Portfolio of 12 Partial Players and does a 3000ms UCT search within HPS"; a Partial Player "computes only a partial move for a turn", grouped "by tactical category or game phase", and the top level iterates the cross product of partial moves. — [HPS, AIIDE 2015](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AIIDE/article/view/12787)
- CherryPi build-order switching: 25 build orders, 42 actions, decisions every 5 s. — [arXiv 1811.08568](https://arxiv.org/abs/1811.08568)
- OpenAI Five: no macro layer; 8,000-80,000 discretized actions per step, with item/ability purchase and courier scripted. — [OpenAI Five](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.06680)
- Wesnoth RCA: 15 candidate actions, each a full expert procedure, arbitrated by fixed scores. — [Wesnoth wiki: RCA AI](https://wiki.wesnoth.org/RCA_AI)

**Intents and macro-goals labelled from human replays**
- HMS attention labels: "We observe that regions where attack takes place can be indicator of players' destination ... we define ground-truth regions as the regions where players conduct their next attack"; a session "ends along with attack behavior", the label for session s−1 is the region y_s attacked at the start of s; output = 144 regions (12×12). Phase labels: the same rule restricted to attacks on "turrets, baron, dragon, and base" (14 major resources). Allies' attention labels are fed as features (imitated communication). — [HMS](https://arxiv.org/abs/1812.07887)
- JueWu-SL: global intent = "the numbered region on the map where the next series of attacks happen" over a 24×24 grid (576 classes), keeping only "regions where continuous attacking behaviors exist"; local intent = a 12×12 local grid cell "extracted from the halfway position of the players between two attack events" (mean interval 6.5 s). Data: top-1% players, filtered by a per-hero performance score, 1/20 of frames kept, "100 million samples from about 0.12 million games ... for one hero". — [JueWu-SL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.12582)
- MGG macro-goals: region intent ("where to go") and resource intent ("what to do"); label g_t = f(s_{t+C}) with C = 30 s and ±3 s noise; 4060-dim state, 64-dim goal; trained with focal loss plus a lineup auto-encoding auxiliary task; rationale: "top players' matches are observed to have high-quality and diverse macro-strategies but noisy micro-operations". — [MGG](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2021/file/86dba86754c0ad93997a11fa947d97b2-Paper.pdf)
- Cicero intents: "a message is defined to have intent z if z is the most likely set of actions that the sender and recipient will take—for both the current turn and several future turns—if no further dialogue occurs after the message is received"; annotation used "a dialogue-conditional action prediction model trained on a 'truthful' subset of the dataset" with dialogue history truncated at the message, because actual played actions "may not reflect the content of a message if (i) a message is not honest or (ii) subsequent messages change the sender's plans". Richer intents were rejected: "harder to annotate ... harder to select with the planning module, and create greater risk of taking the language model out of distribution". — [Cicero TR](https://noambrown.com/papers/22-Science-Diplomacy-TR.pdf)
- Pang et al.: macro-actions "obtained through data mining from trajectories of experts" with PrefixSpan over raw action sequences up to length C, sorted by frequency, duplicates and "meaningless" sequences (selection, camera moves) removed, top K kept; then "since macro actions are not code, we need to 'translate' them into specific code". — [Pang et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1809.09095)

**Option discovery from demonstrations (general RL literature)**
- DDO (Fox et al., arXiv Mar 2017): expectation-gradient fitting of "a fixed, predetermined number of options"; four-rooms: "The hierarchical approaches achieve roughly the same average reward after 1000 episodes as A does after 5000 episodes"; Atari RAM: DQN for 1,000 episodes, 100 greedy trajectories, options discovered, DQN restarted with augmented actions: "significant improvements in 4 out of 5 domains"; surgical segmentation matched annotation at 72%. Hyperparameters (number of options, when to apply) were "tuned ... based on observing the RL agent's performance". — [DDO, arXiv 1703.08294](https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.08294)
- CompILE (Kipf et al., ICML 2019): "a novel unsupervised, fully-differentiable sequence segmentation module" that discovers sub-task boundaries; the hierarchical agent "could learn given only sparse rewards, where agents without task-specific policies struggle" (grid world and continuous control). — [CompILE, arXiv 1812.01483](https://arxiv.org/abs/1812.01483)
- Directed-Info GAIL (ICLR 2019): learns sub-task policies "from unsegmented demonstrations" by maximizing directed information between latent sub-task variables and trajectories. — [arXiv 1810.01266](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.01266)
- Option-GAIL (Jing et al., ICML 2021): EM with option-Viterbi over unsegmented demonstrations; Hopper 3700 vs GAIL 535; Walker2d 4837 vs 2788; AntPush 95 vs 55; CloseMicrowave 100.7 vs 39.1 (GAIL) and 57.0 (Directed-Info GAIL). — [arXiv 2106.05530](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.05530)

### Inferences
- The labels that worked in strategy games are cheap, deterministic functions of the future trajectory (next attack region, state at t+30 s), not latent-variable inference; the latent-option methods have no reported strategy-game result.
- HMS/JueWu-SL needed ~10^8 samples per hero; MGG's meta-controller ~26 GPU-hours on 8 P40s. At 17,000 games the analogous label set is three orders of magnitude smaller, which argues for coarse label spaces (tens of regions, not 576).

### Gaps
- No source reports how label noise (attacks that were incidental) affected the intent model, beyond JueWu-SL's "continuous attacking" filter.
- Pang et al. do not report the final number K of mined macro-actions in the main text.
- No paper was found that infers intents on human demonstrations for a turn-based hex wargame.

## Q3. Portfolio / script-selection search in RTS (Churchill & Buro; Lelis; Ontañón; Barriga): how do they combine exact local search (combat) with higher-level selection, and what were their measured results and costs?

### Takeaway
The portfolio family replaces per-unit action choice by per-unit (or per-type) script choice and evaluates candidates by fast simulator playouts: PGS (hill-climbing, 2013) beat alpha-beta and UCT in 32-50 unit battles at 40 ms; POE (evolution, 2016) beat PGS ~75%; SSS (type-stratified, 2017) beat POE 80-100% at large sizes; asymmetric abstractions (2018) search the full action space for a few units and scripts for the rest; Puppet Search (2017) lifts the same idea to whole-game scripts with choice points; evolved portfolios (2019) beat the 2017 μRTS champion ≥91%; and Prismata's Hierarchical Portfolio Search reached the top 25% of a human ladder in a turn-based game with a 3 s UCT search over 12 partial players.

### Cited Findings

**Portfolio Greedy Search (Churchill & Buro, CIG 2013)**
- Mechanism: "It reduces the number of actions searched for each unit by limiting them to actions produced by a set of scripts called a portfolio; Instead of searching an exponential number of combinations of unit actions, it instead applies a hill-climbing technique to reduce this to a linear amount; It does not perform any recursive tree search, but instead relies on accurate heuristic evaluations at the root node; It improves the quality of heuristic evaluation by performing playouts with individually chosen unit-script assignments". Portfolio = (NOK-AV, Kiter); I = 1 improvement iteration, R = 0 responses "because higher settings do not yet run within 40 ms". — [PGS](https://skatgame.net/mburo/ps/combat13.pdf)
- Results (separated states, score = wins + draws/2, 500 battles per cell): vs Alpha-Beta / AB best-response / UCT / UCT best-response at n = 8: 0.319 / 0.253 / 0.511 / 0.493; n = 16: 0.848 / 0.608 / 0.729 / 0.742; n = 32: 0.952 / 0.932 / 0.888 / 0.900; n = 50: 0.990 / 0.992 / 0.924 / 0.900. All three search methods scored 100% against scripts. In symmetric states an alpha-beta computing a best response to NOK-AV exploited PGS (0.290 at n = 8) because "NOK-AV will be the script chosen by the greedy search the majority of the time". — [PGS](https://skatgame.net/mburo/ps/combat13.pdf)
- Cost: execution time grows quadratically in units (playout cost is linear); the 40 ms limit "was only reached when performing searches on states with more than 2 × 25 units"; SparCraft "can perform millions of unit actions per second". — [PGS](https://skatgame.net/mburo/ps/combat13.pdf)

**Portfolio Online Evolution (Wang, Chen, Li, Holmgård, Togelius, AIIDE 2016)**
- Genome = an m×n matrix of scripts (n units × m = 3 future steps), mutation rate 0.2, playouts limited to 25 rounds, 20 ms budget in JarCraft; POE "is able to win against NOKAV with a 1.0 rate in all battles with army size >= 8", beats PGS "with all army sizes ... Average win rate is around 75%", and beats script- and cluster-based UCT "significantly in large-scale combats where there are more than 32 units" while near 0.5 in very small battles; POE-6 (six scripts) beats POE-2 in all configurations. In their implementation "both UCT-based methods outperform Portfolio Greedy Search in all combat sizes". — [POE](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AIIDE/article/view/12862)
- Limitation: "some of the moves output by POE does not make great sense ... In some tests where a large army size is selected and POE player is given 6 scripts that allow all directions of unit moves at all times, its performance can be reduced greatly." — [POE](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AIIDE/article/view/12862)

**Stratified Strategy Selection (Lelis, IJCAI 2017)**
- "SSS uses a type system to partition the player's units into types and assumes that units of the same type must follow the same strategy"; types from attack range, damage and hp level; scripts {NOKAV, Kiter, Cluster}; 40 ms; SparCraft, 4-56 units per side, 1,000 matches per cell. A corrected evaluation (PGS+: only the first joint action follows the assigned scripts, then NOKAV) already beats PGS and POE; SSS/SSS+ win "usually above 80% in scenarios with more units", e.g. (Zea 8, Dra 8) SSS 92% vs POE; (Zea 14, Dra 14, Ling 14, Mar 14) SSS+ 96% vs POE, 95% vs PGS+, 93% vs SSS (which ran out of time and defaulted units to NOKAV). In the smallest mixed scenario PGS+ beats SSS 57%. — [SSS](https://www.ijcai.org/proceedings/2017/0522.pdf)
- On search vs learning for combat: "strategy selection algorithms tend to scale more easily to large combat scenarios than learning-based methods—while the former can effectively handle battles with more than 100 units, the latter are usually tested on battles with around 50 units." — [SSS](https://www.ijcai.org/proceedings/2017/0522.pdf)

**Asymmetric action abstractions (Moraes & Lelis, AAAI 2018)**
- "asymmetric abstractions restrict the number of moves of only a subset of units"; GAB/SAB run PGS/SSS to a local maximum, then alpha-beta over the full action space of N unrestricted units chosen by attack-value (AV+, N = 4 best); scripts NOKAV and Kiter, 40 ms, 6-56 units per side, 1,000 matches per configuration; GAB vs PGS ~79% and SAB vs SSS ~93% in a 32-unit mixed scenario; a theorem states asymmetric abstractions retain the un-abstracted space's advantage over uniform abstractions. — [arXiv 1711.08101](https://arxiv.org/abs/1711.08101) (ar5iv rendering)

**Puppet Search (Barriga, Stanescu, Buro, AIIDE 2015 / TCIAIG 2017)**
- "an action abstraction mechanism that, given a non-deterministic strategy, works by constantly selecting action choices that dictate how to continue the game based on look-ahead search results"; ABCD or UCTCD over choice points, 100 ms per frame, optional 5 s "standing plan". μRTS Table I (average tournament win rate, 8×8 / 16×16 / 24×24 / BloodBath 64×64 / 8 AIIDE maps): PuppetABCDSingle 68.5 / 66.1 / 68.8 / 79.5 / 83.0; PuppetABCDBasic 22.6 / 41.1 / 52.2 / 75.6 / 88.0; NaiveMCTS 53.7 / 57.4 / 33.9 / 3.0 / 8.2; WorkerRush 76.5 / 55.5 / 30.5 / 30.4 / 10.5. "On small maps the performances of Puppet Search and other search based algorithms are similar ... On the bigger maps, no agent comes close to Puppet Search." — [Puppet Search TCIAIG](https://skatgame.net/mburo/ps/TCIAIG17-puppet.pdf)
- Emergence and design lessons: Puppet Search "will often start with a WorkerRush and later switch to training stronger units and use its previously built workers for gathering resources, thus bypassing the scripts' hard-coded decision"; "PuppetSingle clearly outperforms PuppetBasic on the smaller maps, while the opposite is true on the bigger ones. This exemplifies the importance of designing choice points carefully. They must be potentially useful, otherwise they are just increasing the branching factor of the search tree without providing any benefit." Implementation: ~1,600 lines vs ~3,400 for AHTN. Obstacle in commercial games: "the lack of an accurate forward model or simulator". — [Puppet Search TCIAIG](https://skatgame.net/mburo/ps/TCIAIG17-puppet.pdf)

**Evolved and synthesized portfolios (Lelis group)**
- Mariño et al. 2019 (AAAI): generate a pool Z of 300 strategies by varying parameters of a few rule-based templates (Rush, Defense, Military), then evolve which subset induces the action abstraction as a "subset selection game" (population 20-100, 20-30 generations); PGS*/SSS* "win all their matches against the baselines in maps of size 64×64", the best hand-portfolio baseline (SSSr) wins only 9.5% vs SSS* on 24×24, and both "substantially outperform STT [2017 μRTS champion] in all maps tested, with 91% being the smallest average percentage of victories". — [Mariño 2019, AAAI](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/4072)
- Mariño et al. 2021 (AAAI): LS2 synthesizes a whole strategy script in a DSL (μLanguage, no nested conditionals) by local search with self-play (400 steps; 10 runs each ~30 min on 8×8/9×8/18×8 and 7 h on 24×24, on one 56-core machine); four professional programmers wrote scripts in the same DSL (66 min average); the synthesized script had the highest win rate on two of four maps and a programmer's on the other two; 8×8 averages: LS2 83.9%, GNS 81.9, P2 76.5, WR 75.6, A3N 62.8, NaiveMCTS 56.4, STT 54.1. — [Mariño 2021, AAAI](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/16114)

**Strategy-selection meta-game (Tavares et al., AIIDE 2016)**
- Treats the choice among a pool of AIIDE 2015 bots as a game; "it is useful to deviate from Nash Equilibrium to exploit sub-optimal opponents on strategy selection, confirming insights from computer rock-paper-scissors tournaments" (abstract only; full numbers not retrieved). — [AIIDE 2016](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AIIDE/article/view/12857)

**Turn-based multi-action games**
- Prismata HPS (Churchill & Buro, AIIDE 2015): alternating turns with multiple actions, ~40 units on board, state space ≥10^74; "a bottom-up, two level hierarchical search system inspired by military hierarchical command structure ... At the top layer, all possible combinations of these suggestions are iterated over by a high-level search technique (such as MiniMax or Monte-Carlo tree search)"; leaf evaluation by symmetric playouts. Master bot (12 partial players, 3,000 ms UCT) played ~200 incognito ladder games over 48 h and "achieved a ranking of Tier 6 with 48% progression toward Tier 7 ... within the top 25% of human players", no player noticing. Table 2 averages: UCT100 83.1%, AB100 82.0%, Expert (2-ply alpha-beta) 70.8%, Medium (random portfolio move) 40.3%, Easy 20.5%; Table 3: AB3k 71.8% avg vs UCT100 28.3%, so search time buys strength. — [HPS](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AIIDE/article/view/12787)
- Online Evolution for Hero Academy (Justesen, Mahlmann, Togelius, EvoApplications 2016): 5 action points per turn, ~60 actions available on average, estimated branching 60^5 ≈ 7.8×10^8 per turn; evolving the turn's action sequence with a heuristic fitness at 6 s per turn on one processor: "Online evolution was the best performing method with a minimum winning percentage of 80.5% against the best of the other methods" (MCTS and two greedy searches); ~3,693 generations per turn but it "seems to get stuck at a local optima". — [Justesen 2016](http://julian.togelius.com/Justesen2016Online.pdf)

**Monolithic RL against these portfolios, for calibration**
- Gym-μRTS (Huang et al., 2021): PPO with GridNet/UAS action decomposition, invalid-action masking and shaped rewards (+10 win, +1 harvest, +1 worker, +0.2 building, +1 attack, +4 combat unit), 300M steps on "one GPU, three vCPU, 16GB RAM" in ~60 h; the best agent "can defeat every μRTS bot we tested from the past μRTS competitions when working in a single-map setting" (91% cumulative on 16×16; ~45% vs the MCTS-based Droplet, which "usually defeats our agent by destroying the first barracks"). — [Gym-μRTS, arXiv 2105.13807](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.13807)
- RAISocketAI (2024): "the first DRL agent to win the IEEE microRTS competition" (CoG 2023, 72% overall, 62% vs Mayari); "scripted agents have predominantly won the five previous iterations"; 70 GPU-days total (base model 300M steps on 5 maps, then per-map transfer); 0% vs Mayari and CoacAI on the 64×64 map; a behaviour-cloning start from bot replays cost 23 GPU-days plus 49 GPU-days of PPO. — [arXiv 2402.08112](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.08112)

### Inferences
- All portfolio results hinge on a fast, exact forward model: PGS's 40 ms budget covers ~25 units per side with two scripts, Prismata's 3 s covers a 12-partial-player cross product. A simulator with 0.05 ms steps and an exact per-fight enumerator is in the same regime.
- The strength ordering PGS < POE < PGS+ < SSS shows that most of the gain came from fixing the evaluation (PGS+) and from restricting the assignment space by types, not from more search; the same lesson (choice-point design) appears in Puppet Search.
- Learned components entered this family as evaluation or labelling (Barriga 2017 CNN, Stanescu CNN evaluation cited in Puppet Search), not as replacement of the search.

### Gaps
- Tavares 2016's payoff-matrix and method-by-method win rates were not retrievable (site refused connections).
- Barriga 2017's numeric results (CNN + tactical search vs components) not retrieved.
- No portfolio-search paper reports GPU cost; all are single-CPU-thread with millisecond budgets.

## Q4. Where did hierarchical decompositions fail or cap performance, and what did authors say about the seams between levels (misaligned objectives, non-stationarity)?

### Takeaway
The documented failures are: the top level and sub-policies destabilizing each other when trained together (Pang et al., HIRO's non-stationarity), macro spaces that cap strategy diversity and get exploited (TStarBot1's rush, PGS's best-response exploitability), poorly chosen choice points that only add branching (Puppet Search), learned hierarchies that add nothing over exploration (Nachum et al.) or nothing at all (wargaming HRL 2024), and hand-scripted parts that authors expect to be suboptimal but kept for cost (OpenAI Five, Cicero's intent expressiveness).

### Cited Findings
- Simultaneous training instability: "if controller and all sub-policies are still updated at the same time, the training is sometimes unstable due to the mutual influence of different networks ... every network would see the other networks as the part of environment, and do not consider the changes of the others while updating. These unstable training rarely happened on lower difficulty levels, but can be seen on higher difficulty levels. We can use an alternate updating strategy to train the networks which can result in a steady improvement, but need more samples." — [Pang et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1809.09095)
- HIRO's formulation of the seam: "Since the lower-level policy is changing underneath the higher-level policy, a sample observed for a certain high-level action in the past may not yield the same low-level behavior in the future", "a non-stationary problem for the higher-level policy"; fixed by relabelling past high-level goals (10 candidates) to the one most likely to reproduce the observed low-level actions; results at 10M steps: Ant Maze 0.99 ± 0.01, Ant Push 0.92 ± 0.04, Ant Fall 0.66 ± 0.07, while "FeUdal Networks variants: nearly 0.0 on most tasks" and option-critic "failed to produce reasonable performance". — [HIRO, arXiv 1805.08296](https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.08296) (ar5iv rendering)
- Nachum et al. (2019): "most of the observed benefits of hierarchy can be attributed to improved exploration, as opposed to easier policy learning or imposed hierarchical structures"; non-hierarchical "Explore & Exploit" and "Switching Ensemble" agents reach "performance competitive with hierarchical RL while at the same time being much simpler to use and implement". — [arXiv 1909.10618](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.10618)
- Learned options in games: FeUdal reports that "The experimental results for Option-Critic on 4 ATARI games show scores similar those from a flat DQN baseline agent", whereas FuN "doubles it on Ms. Pacman, more than triples it on Zaxxon and gets more than 20x improvement on Asterix"; FuN's own ablations: a Manager without dilation "fails catastrophically" and a Manager horizon of 1 hurts; the LSTM baseline on Montezuma needed "broaden[ed] and intensif[ied] ... hyper-parameter search ... to see any progress at all". — [FeUdal, arXiv 1703.01161](https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.01161); [option-critic, arXiv 1609.05140](https://arxiv.org/abs/1609.05140)
- Macro space capping strategy: TStarBot1 "lacks strategy diversity in order to consistently beat human players ... will lose consistently once a human player figures out TStarBot1's preference for Zergling Rush"; causes named: "A lack of opponent diversity" and "A lack of deep exploration. The production of advanced units are buried down very deeply in the tech tree". Also the flat macro controller's design cost: "a single controller has to work over all action groups, where the actions of different groups are mutually exclusive ... the macro action does not have any control over individual units". — [TStarBots](https://arxiv.org/abs/1809.07193)
- Exploitability of script-restricted search: PGS is beaten by an alpha-beta best response to its dominant script in symmetric states (score 0.290 at n = 8); SSS's author: "even if SSS had unlimited time to compute a strategy, an opponent that considers all legal actions during search might be able to exploit player i, as SSS is constrained to the unit actions returned by the scripts." — [PGS](https://skatgame.net/mburo/ps/combat13.pdf); [SSS](https://www.ijcai.org/proceedings/2017/0522.pdf)
- Evaluation-level misalignment between the assignment and the playout: PGS's playout "assumes that the units will follow the scripts defined in b_i in every decision point to the end of the match. This means that one assumes that units following the Cluster script will not attack for the rest of the match, which is unreasonable", so PGS never selected the non-offensive script; fixing the playout (PGS+) reversed the PGS-vs-POE ordering. — [SSS](https://www.ijcai.org/proceedings/2017/0522.pdf)
- Bad choice points: "They must be potentially useful, otherwise they are just increasing the branching factor of the search tree without providing any benefit"; a standing plan helps only on big maps, and NaiveMCTS "cannot scale to the larger maps" (3.0% on BloodBath). — [Puppet Search TCIAIG](https://skatgame.net/mburo/ps/TCIAIG17-puppet.pdf)
- Hierarchy that did not help: the 2024 wargaming HRL/scripted hybrid's "initial tests did not show improved performance". — [arXiv 2408.13333](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.13333)
- Cost-driven scripting at the top: OpenAI Five "believe[s] the agent could ultimately perform better if these actions were not scripted"; HMS's supervised agent "did not have advantage on teamfight over human teams" and its turret lead "shrank due to weaker teamfight ability"; JueWu-SL's authors note "our AI is the collection of intelligence from top human players ... trained to synthesize an averaged strategy from human gameplays". — [OpenAI Five](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.06680); [HMS](https://arxiv.org/abs/1812.07887); [JueWu-SL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.12582)
- Human-anchored planning trades strength for humanness explicitly: in piKL, larger λ "keeps the learned policy closer to the anchor", accuracy ~43% at λ = 10^-1 with score ~16.5% versus ~41% accuracy and ~18.5% at λ = 10^-3; Cicero used a smaller λ (3 × 10^-3) for its own best response than for modelling others "allowing Cicero more leeway to deviate when the action that it predicted humans would most likely choose in its situation was suboptimal". — [piKL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.07544); [Cicero TR](https://noambrown.com/papers/22-Science-Diplomacy-TR.pdf)
- Learned strategy selection overfits its training opponents: CherryPi's gains on training bots did not transfer (0.635 and 0.706 on held-out bots vs 0.878-0.888) and "gains for variants with memory observations on the training bots can not be transferred to held-out bots". — [arXiv 1811.08568](https://arxiv.org/abs/1811.08568)
- Monolithic RL fails to generalize across map scale: RAISocketAI "learns behaviors on a smaller size scale and is not able to generalize behaviors such as resource gathering or enemy pursuit to larger maps"; 0% vs prior winners on 64×64. — [arXiv 2402.08112](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.08112)

### Inferences
- Two seams recur: (1) the top level's training signal is defined relative to a moving low level (HIRO, Pang), which the deployed systems avoid by freezing one side (imitation-trained macro in HMS/JueWu-SL, fixed anchor in Cicero, rule-based micro in TStarBot1); (2) the low-level expert set bounds what the top level can express (TStarBot1's rush, PGS/SSS's script restriction), which search-based systems mitigate by unrestricted units (asymmetric abstractions) or by evolving the portfolio.
- Nachum's result implies that a learned hierarchy on top of a strong imitation prior should not be expected to help by itself; the benefit must come from the experts encoding exact computation (combat outcomes, reachability) the prior cannot.

### Gaps
- No paper quantifies the strength lost to scripted sub-modules (OpenAI Five's item scripting, TStarBot1's hit-and-run micro); the statements are qualitative.
- No source measured how often an imitation-trained macro layer issues a target that the micro layer cannot execute (the "unreachable intent" failure) in a strategy game.

## Q5. Which of these are feasible on tens of GPU-dollars per experiment for a turn-based hex wargame with an exact simulator, an exact per-fight enumerator, a strong imitation seed from 17,000 human games, and sparse win/loss outcomes?

### Takeaway
On the evidence, the affordable and most-supported transfers are (a) KL-regularized turn-level search anchored to the imitation seed (piKL: +25 points of population score over the IL policy at inference time, no training), (b) portfolio/hierarchical-portfolio turn construction with the exact simulator as playout engine (Prismata: top 25% of a human ladder with one CPU thread), (c) supervised intent labels mined from the 17k replays as an auxiliary head (HMS/JueWu-SL: hours on a few GPUs at 10^8 samples), and (d) a learned selector over a small script/portfolio set (CherryPi: 42 actions); end-to-end hierarchical RL (FeUdal, option-critic, HIRO-style) and goal-conditioned RL at MGG scale are out of budget and weakly supported in games.

### Cited Findings
- Inference-time regularized search needs only a policy, a value and candidate actions: piKL's IL policy scored 7.9% ± 0.4% and piKL-HedgeBot 32.9% ± 0.7% in the same population; chess/Go gains from MCTS on top of an imitation model were +1.1 and +0.7 points of move-prediction accuracy with ">90% winrate against raw models". — [piKL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.07544)
- Human-anchored self-play RL for the value function kept play human-compatible where unanchored self-play collapsed against non-self opponents (DORA 13% vs Diplodocus 27-29%). — [Diplodocus](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.05492)
- Portfolio search costs: PGS at 40 ms handles ~25 units per side on one 2013 CPU core; Prismata's Master bot runs 3,000 ms UCT over 12 partial players and search time monotonically buys strength (AB3k 71.8% avg vs UCT100 28.3%); Online Evolution beat MCTS ≥80.5% in a 5-action-per-turn game at 6 s per turn. — [PGS](https://skatgame.net/mburo/ps/combat13.pdf); [HPS](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AIIDE/article/view/12787); [Justesen 2016](http://julian.togelius.com/Justesen2016Online.pdf)
- Supervised macro labels: HMS trained on 250M instances in ~12 h on 8 GPUs; JueWu-SL used ~100M samples per hero from ~0.12M games; MGG's meta-controller ~26 h on 8 P40s. — [HMS](https://arxiv.org/abs/1812.07887); [JueWu-SL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.12582); [MGG](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2021/file/86dba86754c0ad93997a11fa947d97b2-Paper.pdf)
- Learned selector over scripts: CherryPi's LSTM over 42 build orders trained on 2.8M generated games (compute unstated) gave +8.5 to +9.5 points on training opponents and +0 to −16 on held-out ones. — [arXiv 1811.08568](https://arxiv.org/abs/1811.08568)
- Monolithic RL price tags on a small RTS: ~60 GPU-hours (1 GPU, 3 vCPU) to beat all past μRTS bots on one 16×16 map; 70 GPU-days to win the competition across maps; 23 + 49 GPU-days for a BC-then-PPO variant. — [Gym-μRTS](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.13807); [RAISocketAI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.08112)
- Macro-action RL against built-in AIs: TStarBot1 needed 3,840 CPUs for 1-2 days and ~7M games to reach level 10; Pang et al. used 4 GPUs + 48 CPU threads for "days". — [TStarBots](https://arxiv.org/abs/1809.07193); [Pang et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1809.09095)
- End-to-end HRL evidence in games is Atari-scale (FeUdal) and its benefit is attributed to exploration (Nachum), with option-critic at flat-DQN level; goal-conditioned RL with human macro-goals (MGG) ran on 830 V100s for 420 h. — [FeUdal](https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.01161); [Nachum 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.10618); [MGG](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2021/file/86dba86754c0ad93997a11fa947d97b2-Paper.pdf)
- A wargame-specific attempt at RL-over-scripts reported no gain in initial tests. — [arXiv 2408.13333](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.13333)
- In Wesnoth itself, a learned recruiter inside the RCA candidate-action structure reached 66.6-69.3% against the default recruiter after ~1-2k training games on 2012 hardware. — [Wesnoth wiki: ML Recruiter](https://wiki.wesnoth.org/Machine_Learning_Recruiter)

### Inferences (speculative transfers, marked)
- Speculative: piKL over turn plans. Diplomacy's piKL enumerates candidate joint actions per player from the BC policy; a Wesnoth analogue would sample K turn plans from the seed (the whole side-turn as one macro action), score them with the seed's value head after opponent-response sampling, and pick argmax of value + λ·log τ. This costs K forward passes per turn per candidate opponent reply at inference and no training; its acceptance test is the existing 800-game match. The λ sweep in piKL (10^-1 keeps human accuracy, 10^-3 maximizes score) is the analogue of the end_turn offset sweep already run in this project.
- Speculative: hierarchical portfolio search for the side-turn. Prismata is the closest published analogue (turn-based, dozens of units, actions decomposed by category, one CPU thread). A Wesnoth portfolio could be the RCA candidate actions themselves plus seed-sampled plans as "partial players", with the exact combat enumerator as the leaf evaluator; expected cost is CPU-only per game, and the reported quadratic-in-units playout cost applies.
- Speculative: intent auxiliary heads from replays. The HMS/JueWu-SL rule ("region of the next attacks") transfers directly to hex maps at a coarse grid; with 17k games the label count is ~10^5-10^6 side-turns, three orders of magnitude below HMS, so the head should be coarse (tens of regions) and evaluated only by match, since the project already found holdout CE is not a strength proxy.
- Speculative: learned selection over a few experts (CherryPi pattern). A 10-40-way selector trained by outcome prediction on generated games is cheap, but the Gehring result warns that its gain is against the training opponents and shrinks on unseen ones; with self-play as the only opponent source, that is the same non-stationarity seam.
- Not feasible at this budget: MGG-style goal-conditioned RL, FeUdal/HIRO-style learned hierarchies, TStarBot1-scale macro-action RL (thousands of CPU-days).

### Gaps
- No published system combines an imitation-learned turn policy with KL-regularized search in a two-player hex wargame; the piKL transfer is untested.
- No source reports portfolio-search results with a learned prior as one of the portfolio members (all portfolios are hand scripts or evolved scripts).
- Compute for Cicero's/Diplodocus's RL and for CherryPi's training was not found, so their dollar costs cannot be stated.
- No hex-wargame HRL paper with numbers was found; the 2024 wargaming HRL abstract reports no improvement and gives no budget.
